# Legal review brief: Watazawwado terms and privacy policy

Prepared 2026-10-16 for a one-time review by a lawyer. Not legal advice; these are the points we want checked.

## The business

- Online 1-on-1 lessons (Quran, Islamic studies, Arabic, English), live on Zoom, sold to consumers, mostly families in the EU, UK and North America. Many learners are children; a parent books and pays.
- Operator: Mahmoud Elwany (legal form and registration: owner to fill in). Governing law in the terms: Poland. Website: https://watazawwado.academy
- Live documents: `/terms` (version 2026-10-15) and `/privacy`. Every booking and package purchase records which terms version the customer accepted.
- Payments: Stripe and PayPal, paid in advance. Prices: $9 per hour (Quran/Islamic studies), from $13 per hour (Arabic/English). Prepaid packages of 4, 8 or 12 lessons.

## Questions for the lawyer

1. **Seller identification.** The terms do not state the trader's full name, postal address, tax/registration number (NIP/CEIDG) or a complaints email. What must be added to comply with the Consumer Rights Directive and Polish law?
2. **14-day right of withdrawal (distance contracts).**
   - Single lessons are often booked for a date within 14 days. Do we need an explicit checkbox ("I ask you to start before the withdrawal period ends and I understand I lose the right once the lesson is delivered") and a model withdrawal form?
   - **Packages**: unused lessons are "not refunded automatically, reviewed case by case". Within 14 days of purchase, must unused package lessons be refunded on request? What should the terms say after 14 days?
3. **Refund limit.** Lessons cancelled 3+ hours ahead become a lesson credit; a cash refund is offered instead, limited to 3 lessons per account. Is this cap enforceable against consumers, or must it be reworded?
4. **Late cancellation / no-show** counts as a delivered lesson (no refund or credit). Acceptable as a consumer term?
5. **Governing law and courts.** Terms choose Polish law and Polish courts, while saying consumers keep their home-country mandatory protections and may sue at home. Is this wording adequate?
6. **EU ODR platform.** The terms point EU residents to the European Online Dispute Resolution platform. We understand the platform was discontinued in 2025; confirm and tell us what replaces this sentence.
7. **Complaints procedure.** Is a stated deadline for answering complaints required (for example 14 days under Polish law)?
8. **Liability cap** ("limited to the amount paid for that lesson, except where the law does not allow this"). Acceptable?
9. **Children.**
   - Terms: the booking parent is responsible. Privacy: users under 18 act with parental consent. Is that enough under GDPR Art. 8 (Poland: 16) for the accounts and for the AI learning chat?
   - Lessons with minors happen on Zoom with one adult teacher. Any safeguarding statement we should publish?
10. **Privacy policy (GDPR).** Please check that it names the controller with an address, lists the legal basis per purpose, retention periods, all rights (including portability, objection and the right to complain to the supervisory authority, UODO), and international transfers (Supabase, Vercel, Stripe, PayPal, Google Gemini AI, Brevo email, WhatsApp, Zoom, Google Calendar). The policy currently promises deletion "within 48 hours"; is a softer commitment (within one month, as GDPR allows) wiser?
11. **AI.** A Gemini model chats with the student to suggest a learning plan; a teacher reviews any non-standard price. Any disclosure needed (EU AI Act transparency, GDPR automated decision-making)?
12. **Teachers.** Other teachers can be added to the platform. Do we need a separate agreement with them (independent contractor, data processing, conduct with minors)?

## What we need back

- Required changes to the terms and privacy policy (exact wording if possible).
- Anything that must change in the booking or checkout flow (checkboxes, emails, confirmations).
