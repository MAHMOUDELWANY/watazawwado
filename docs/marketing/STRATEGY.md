## 0. The strategy on one page

- **Goal for the first 90 days (to ~12 Feb 2027, Ramadan):** awareness and trust. People know the name, the colours,
  the zaad bag and the founder's face, and start talking about us. Rough paid-learner figure 30–50, which is the two
  teachers' full capacity. [D]
- **North-star number:** waitlist sign-ups ("Pack your zaad"). [D]
- **Who:** parents booking for children up to 17, and young adults 18–35 booking for themselves; US, Canada, UK,
  Europe. [D]
- **The enemy:** "the stranger": you don't know your teacher, and your teacher doesn't know you. [D]
- **The promise:** "Not just lessons. A mentor who stays." [D]
- **The face:** an academy-led brand; Ustadh Mahmoud is its recurring human face as founder and teacher. [D]
- **The offer:** $10/h Quran and Islamic studies, $13/h languages; wave 1 (20 learners) gets 15% off for 12 months;
  wave 1 opens mid-December to early January. [D]
- **The engine:** daily posting on every platform from five pillars; the owner films 1–2 days a week; Claude edits and
  produces the rest. [D]

## 1. Customer psychology and buying motives

Evidence: the owner's memory of families who stayed with him (second-hand, small, survivorship bias) and the
2026-10-19 desk research. Replies to the VOC messages (sent the weekend of 14–15 Nov) will confirm or change this.

### Parents (mostly mothers)
| | |
|---|---|
| Job to be done | "Give my child a real relationship with the deen without me having to chase anyone." |
| Before (push) | "I drop him in class and hope someone is paying attention." Fear the child gets bored or pushed away; no news; teacher changes; nobody knows what the child already knows |
| After (pull) | "He's in safe hands. Someone is watching with me." Families' words: "safe hands", "like his big brother" |
| Anxieties | Who is this teacher? Will it be harsh or rote? Will my child keep going after a month? Is it safe online? |
| Habit (why they don't switch) | The masjid or current institute is free/familiar; switching feels like effort |
| What moves her | Seeing the teacher before booking; being contacted first; a report she doesn't have to ask for |

### Young adults 18–35
| | |
|---|---|
| Job to be done | "Come back to my deen (or learn Arabic for my family) in a way that fits my life and doesn't make me feel judged." |
| Before | "I drifted. If I go back, I'll be judged or lectured." |
| After [H] | "Someone my age gets me and walks with me. No lectures." |
| Anxieties | Being judged for what they don't know; wasting money on something they drop |
| Habit | YouTube and apps (free, private) or "later" |
| What moves them | Relatable tone, humour, privacy of 1-on-1, a small first step |

**Key insight (busy-parent paradox) [R]:** most parents do not follow up, so a dashboard full of data is not the
promise (it is more work). The promise is "you don't have to chase anyone: we come to you"; the dashboard and the
report are the proof.

## 2. Competitive positioning and differentiation

Positioning method: April Dunford, *Obviously Awesome* (position against what the customer would really do instead).

| Alternative | Wins on | Fails on | Our angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| YouTube and apps (young adults) | Free, any time, private | Nobody knows you, corrects you or notices when you stop | A teacher your age who knows you and notices when you drift |
| Doing nothing (young adults) | No effort, no judgment | The distance grows | A small first step with no judgment |
| Masjid or local halaqa (parents) | Free, community, trusted | Group class, fixed times, little news for parents | **Alongside the masjid, not against it** |
| Another online academy (parents) | Same format, often cheaper | Teacher assigned blindly and swapped; reports forgotten | You choose your teacher with a real profile; the teacher stays; we contact you first; a report after every class |

**Unique attributes we can prove today:** choose your own teacher with a visible profile; the same teacher stays
(honest wording, `BRAND.md` §3); a mandatory report after every class; direct teacher–family chat; placement test and
goals at sign-up; our own curriculum; an ijazah-holding founder.

**Rules [R]:** never name an academy; never criticise masjids or weekend schools; attack the practice ("the
stranger"), not a competitor; price is not our edge (we are mid-market: $10/h = $0.167/min in a $0.10–0.27 band).

## 3. Brand story and core messaging

### StoryBrand / STORIFY spine
- **Someone (hero):** the mother who wants her child close to the deen; the young adult who wants to come back.
- **Tension:** worry and guilt (parents); distance and fear of judgment (young adults).
- **Obstacle (villain):** the stranger: an assigned teacher nobody knows, a lecture that pushes people away.
- **Risk:** a child who grows up thinking the deen is boring or harsh; an adult who never comes back.
- **Intervention (guide):** a teacher who knows you, chosen by you, who stays and reaches out first.
- **Future you:** "my son actually looks forward to class"; "for the first time a teacher really knows me".

### Message hierarchy [D]
1. Brand line, fixed everywhere: **"Not just lessons. A mentor who stays."**
2. Enemy: **"Nobody should learn their deen from a stranger."** (Arabic avoids «غريب»: positive in Islam.)
3a. Parents: **"You choose your child's teacher. We message you before you have to ask."**
3b. Young adults: **"Someone your age who gets you. No lectures."**
4. Proof: ijazah (Hafs 'an 'Asim), 100+ students in person and ~25 online, a report after every class, a learner
   with him since May 2026.

### Voice
Warm, plain, a little humour (never about religion), speaks *to* the viewer like a friend, short sentences, one idea
per piece. Concrete over abstract (Made to Stick): every message has a scene a five-year-old could picture.
Information becomes a story: not "we send reports" but "it's Tuesday, 6 pm, your phone buzzes: 'Yusuf nailed the
qalqalah today.'"

### What we say vs. what only customers say
We promise actions (we never swap your teacher, we report after every class, we reach out first). Outcomes ("my
child changed") only ever come from customers in their own words, with permission.

### Three sample scripts (STORIFY, 30–45 s; humanizer and mahmoud-voice before filming)

**A. "The stranger" (parents, awareness, talking to camera, home)**
- Hook: "Quick question. Who's teaching your child the Quran? Not the academy. The person."
- Tension: "Most parents I taught couldn't tell me. The institute picked someone free that week."
- Obstacle/Risk: "And when the teacher doesn't know your child, they teach everyone the same way. Kids notice. They
  switch off."
- Intervention: "So here, you choose the teacher. You see who they are before the first lesson. And they stay."
- Future: "Your child gets someone who knows their name, their pace, and that they love dinosaurs."
- Close: "Nobody should learn their deen from a stranger. Pack your zaad." (zipper gesture) CTA: comment ZAAD.

**B. "We text you first" (parents, trust, screen recording on demo data + voice)**
- Scene: a phone buzzes. "Tuesday, 6:40 pm. 'Salam! Yusuf missed two homework tasks this week. Is everything OK at
  home? I'll keep today's lesson light.'"
- Line: "Parents told me this message mattered more than any certificate. Because they didn't have to ask."
- Proof: the report screen. Close: "A mentor who stays. Pack your zaad."

**C. "No lecture" (young adults, awareness, walking in the street)**
- Hook: "If you've drifted from the deen, I'm not here to lecture you."
- Tension: "Most of us didn't leave. We just got busy, and going back feels awkward."
- Intervention: "Start where you are. One surah. One letter. Someone your age, one-on-one, no audience."
- Light humour: "Worst case? You learn one letter properly. I've seen worse Tuesdays."
- Close: "Pack your zaad." CTA: link in bio.

## 8. Conversion funnel and performance KPIs [D]

Funnel: content → profile → "comment ZAAD" / link in bio → waitlist (me / my child) → invitation (72 h) → sign-up
and choose the teacher → trial + same-day report → first paid lesson → stays 3 months → review or referral.

| Stage | KPI | When |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Reach/views, 3-second hold, average view duration | Weekly |
| Name recall | Searches for "Watazawwado" (YouTube, Google), comments with the name or "zaad", saves and shares | Weekly |
| Trust | WhatsApp and DMs, profile → site clicks, waitlist sign-ups by track (north star) | Weekly |
| Conversion (after the wave opens) | Invitation → sign-up, trial → paid, still learning after 4 weeks | Every 2 weeks |
| Discipline | Days posted (target 90%+), days of content in the bank (target 14) | Weekly |

No numeric targets before a 2-week baseline. Logged in the Notion Weekly Numbers.

## 9. Risks

1. **Owner overload** (warehouse job, studies, filming, teaching). Mitigation: the 2-week bank, Claude does production,
   voice-free formats.
2. **Demand beyond capacity.** Mitigation: waves, an honest waitlist, trusted former colleagues as possible teachers
   (not confirmed).
3. **The brand leans on one face.** Mitigation: every piece says every teacher here works the same way; pillar 5 shows
   the system, not the person.
4. **Thin evidence.** Mitigation: VOC round 1 now, interviews with wave 1 families after their trial.
5. **Religious accuracy.** Unchanged red lines: content RULES §1 (verified text, no music, no fatwas).
