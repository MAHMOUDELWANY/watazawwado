# Our nasheed (draft for owner review)

Status: **[Proposed 2026-11-20]**. Owner's idea: one nasheed that belongs to us, in Arabic and English, used under
carousel videos, at the end of YouTube lessons and courses, and later at events. Rules: `RULES.md` §1.6 (male lead,
children may join the chorus, rhythm first, light instruments very low, our own lyrics, no ayah or hadith sung, commercial-use plan,
stops before recitation).

**Concept (owner, 2026-11-20):** an anthem for the world we are building, like a national anthem in meaning, for all ages.
Belonging, pride and resolve, not a sad or purely religious song. The rhythm and the voice should make people want to
learn it by heart.

## What we make

| Cut | Length | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Sting | ~5 s | "Pack your zaad" / «جهّز زادك» + one daf hit. End cards, intros, transitions |
| Bed | 30 s, loopable, voices low, few words | Under carousel videos and motion pieces (fades out over the last 3 s) |
| Full | 60–90 s | End of long YouTube lessons and courses, events, the homepage film later |

Arabic and English versions of each. Same melody in both, so the sound is recognisable in either language.

## Lyric rules (owner's taste, 2026-11-20; apply to every line, both languages)

1. **Strong words, not just a nice meaning.** Every word must be fresh and weighty. No filler, no general or overused
   words, no stiff archaic phrases (rejected: «ليس تبلى», «إرث آباء كرام», «جسرًا بين ألوان الكلام»).
2. **Every line must make sense on its own.** Rejected: «يعرف اسمي قبل حرفي» (no clear meaning).
3. **Rhyme must sound identical to the ear**, including the vowel before the rhyme letter: «ـَيْنا» (التقَيْنا،
   مضَيْنا، انحنَيْنا) never rhymes with «ـِينا» (رقِينا). Check every rhyme aloud.
4. **The metre must sing evenly.** Arabic: check every line against the feet (here مجزوء الرمل: فاعلاتن فاعلاتن). A
   line that breaks the metre is rewritten, even if the owner suggested it, and the reason is explained.
5. **Few words per line.** Wordy lines break the tune (rejected: "who holds your hand the whole way through"). English
   lines about 6–8 syllables, four beats.
6. **English is its own song**, never a translation of the Arabic: its own images, clean rhymes, punchy wording.
7. **An anthem for everyone.** Resolve, pride and energy that make you want to move a mountain; not sad, not purely
   religious. Do not narrow it to one group or list family members (rejected: "to every daughter, every son"); the
   family appears once only (the home stanza).
8. The academy speaks as "we"; never centre one person.
9. **Feedback is about the spirit, not the letter.** When the owner rejects a line, rethink the whole stanza fresh;
   do not patch the one phrase and keep the same idea (rejected: dropping "daughter, son" but keeping "pass it on").
   Keep what he praised (e.g. "Before the sun, a small voice rose").
10. **No repetition inside a line or across the song** (rejected: "our zaad is light, our zaad is truth"); vary key
    words (light, road, way) between stanzas.
12. **Simple, everyday English.** Words any listener knows, including non-native speakers (rejected: "morning dew");
    no rare or poetic vocabulary.
11. **Self-review before showing:** read every line aloud for rhythm; if a line is only "fine", rewrite it.

### English (v10 draft for owner review; resolve approved)

**[Chorus]**
We rise as one from every shore,
together, stronger than before;
we pack our zaad, we take the road,
and carry light, and share the load.

**[Resolve]** (approved)
Our will is stone, it will not break,
we will not bend, we will not shake;
and if a mountain blocks the way,
with faith we climb it, come what may.

**[1: home]**
At dawn, a child begins to read,
and every word becomes a seed;
we plant it here, and it will grow
in Jannah, more than we can know.

**[2: language]**
The Quran's tongue runs in our veins,
a pulse, a flame that still remains;
we lift it high with pride and grace
and let it shine in every place.

**[3: the teacher]**
A friend to walk the path with you,
who sees the best in all you do;
and when you fall, he's at your side,
he lifts you up, he is your guide.

Melody reference (owner, 2026-11-20): he likes the rhythm and tune of "Whisper", the PUBG MOBILE Primewood Genesis
theme song (https://youtu.be/APtV8Je_cl4), for youth and children. Measured 2026-11-20 from the audio (3:15):
about 110 bpm, possibly with a lilting triplet feel; a sung melody with gliding notes in the middle register; heavy
bass and drums under it; shape: soft opening that builds (0:00-0:48), a short drop, a fuller section, an almost silent
break around 1:26, the biggest section 1:35-2:40, a second break around 2:50, then a fade.
**What we take** (mood, pace and shape only, never the melody): ~110 bpm with a gentle lilt; a soft, close, almost
whispered solo voice at the start; layers of voices added section by section; one quiet break before the last chorus
where only the voice remains; a soft ending. **What we replace:** bass and drums are instruments, so the weight comes
from a low male choir on long vowels and a daf with hand claps. We describe its mood and tempo in our own words only: ElevenLabs refuses song or
artist names, and copying a melody is not allowed.

## Generation plan (to save ElevenLabs credits, which also voice the curriculum)

1. **One test first:** the Arabic chorus only, about 20 s, one take, model `music_v2_5` (ElevenLabs' recommended
   model). Styles in English, lyrics with full tashkeel (ElevenLabs: "styles must be in English, lyrics can be any
   language"). Their docs say nothing about Arabic pronunciation, so this test decides whether the model can do it.
2. **Style words:** "a cappella", "soft intimate male vocals", "gentle daf", "slow", "quiet", "sparse"; "no instruments,
   no piano, no synths, no strings".
3. **Quiet background version: no extra credits.** We lower the volume ourselves when we mix it under a video
   (about -18 dB under the voice), and cut the 5 s sting and the 30 s bed from the full take.
4. If the test still changes the vowels: stop spending credits. Options then: the owner (or a munshid) records the
   Arabic vocals, and we keep the AI only for the English version.
5. ElevenLabs Music terms (09 Oct 2026): our Creator plan allows online and offline commercial use and streaming, no
   attribution needed, 62 minutes of music a month; we stay responsible for checking each output.
