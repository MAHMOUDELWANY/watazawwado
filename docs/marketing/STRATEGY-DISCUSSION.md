## Confirmed decisions
1. **[2026-11-12] Academy-led brand, the owner as its recurring human face** (founder and teacher), not a personal
   brand. Why: people trust a real person behind a logo, but a personal brand creates demand for one teacher who caps
   at ~25 learners, and the owner plans to keep a small group and later step back to management.
2. **[2026-11-12] Two primary audiences**, across the US, Canada, UK and Europe:
   - **Parents booking for children up to 17**, English-speaking, many from immigrant families with some Arab culture;
     Arab diaspora parents (content in Arabic) are included.
   - **Young adults ~18–35 booking for themselves**: coming back to the deen in a way that fits them, or learning
     Arabic because their family is from an Arab country.
   - Lowest priority: adults over ~35–40. Why: the two teachers are young and understand this age group.
3. **[2026-11-12] One enemy: "the stranger".** You don't know your teacher (assigned by an institute), and your
   teacher doesn't know you (so he lectures and pushes you away). Opposite of "a mentor who stays". English working
   line: "Nobody should learn their deen from a stranger." The Arabic avoids «غريب» (positive in Islam: طوبى
   للغرباء). We attack the practice, never a named academy. To check against the VOC replies.
5. **[2026-11-12] Wave 1 opens between mid-December 2026 and early January 2027**, flexible: earlier if the content
   brings real demand, later if not. Why: the owner lost his voice (throat infection) and filming has not started.
   Content comes first; the plan is reviewed on results. This window falls in Rajab–Sha'ban, the pre-Ramadan
   deciding season (PLAN.md §6).
6. **[2026-11-12] Production model.** The owner works in a warehouse and studies (job interview for flexible part-time
   work 16–17 Nov). He gives **1–2 full filming days a week**, on his phone: talking-to-camera "yapping" videos,
   some walking in the street, some at home or at the desk (screen recordings of the platform's progress). He reviews
   every script (may edit it himself in Notion without telling Claude, so Claude always reads the latest version
   before producing) and answers comments himself. **Claude edits** (light editing, few effects) and produces the
   rest: motion-graphics videos with his voice clone (the existing video skill, to be improved), carousels and written
   posts. Goal: **post daily on every platform**. Later task: an editing skill for the two filmed formats (street and
   home), from the owner's references.
7. **[2026-11-12] No "AI" label on voice-clone videos.** Owner: the clone is his own voice, the script is his.
   Claude raised the trust risk once; the owner decided. Only exception: where a platform's upload form itself
   requires the synthetic-media toggle, follow the platform's rule.
4. **[2026-11-12] Use proven external frameworks** (books, research) when setting rules, not only the owner's input;
   each one adapted and labelled with its source and limits.

## Voice of customer (owner's memory of past families, 2026-11-12)
Evidence level: second-hand, from the owner's recollection of families who stayed with him at other academies
(survivorship bias, small number). Strong enough for hypotheses, not for claims.
- **Who:** US and Canada, children speak English only, immigrant families with some Arab culture; ages 8 to early 20s.
- **What families complained about (other teachers and academies):** no follow-up, parents drop the child in class and
  nobody watches progress; reports missing, forgotten or sent loosely on WhatsApp; the teacher does not know what the
  learner wants; repeating what the learner already knows (boredom, nothing new); weak English, no real conversation;
  memorized but not understood; unreliable teachers.
- **Why they stayed with him:** honest and deeply caring; contacted parents directly and early, at the first signs of
  neglect, before a problem grew; detailed reports and advice; opened class with personal talk and humour; handled
  cheating (hidden Mushaf, a recitation tab) with wisdom and a joke, not a lecture; kept answering unexpected
  questions in a second language instead of freezing.
- **Families did not choose their teacher:** they joined the institute, which assigned whichever teacher was free;
  they knew nothing about the teacher (background, qualifications) until the trial, while the institute promised
  "top teachers". This supports our edge #2 (learners pick a real teacher at sign-up, with a visible profile).
- **Families' own words (as remembered):** "my son is in safe hands", "you're like his big brother".
- **What the platform already turns into a system:** goals asked at sign-up, placement test, mandatory report right
  after class (teacher + parent/learner), direct teacher–learner–family chat.
- **Hiring principle (owner):** "A teacher is born, not made." Patient, adaptable, flexible, calm under a hard
  question, loves teaching; very selective hiring.

## Competitive alternatives (owner, 2026-11-12; analysis is Claude's hypothesis)
| Audience | Real alternative | What it does well | Where it fails them | Our angle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Young adults | YouTube and apps | Free, any time, private (no one judges) | Nobody knows you, nobody corrects your recitation, nobody notices when you stop | A teacher your age who knows you, corrects you kindly and notices when you drift; still private (1-on-1) |
| Young adults | Doing nothing, "later" | No effort, no risk of feeling judged | Time passes; the distance grows | A first step that is small and free of judgment |
| Parents | Masjid or local halaqa | Free or cheap, community, in person, trusted locally | Group class: the child gets lost; fixed times and driving; little news for parents; may be rote or harsh | **Alongside the masjid, not against it**: one teacher who knows your child, from home, a report after every class |
| Parents | Another online academy or institute | Same format, often cheaper or bigger | Teacher assigned blindly and swapped; reports forgotten; promises vs reality | Choose your own teacher with a real profile; the same teacher stays; we contact you first; a report every class |

Rule candidate: never criticise masjids or weekend schools (community trust, and many families use both). Position
as a complement.

## Strategic hypotheses
- A rough 30–50 paid learners in 90 days is the two teachers' full capacity, so content should build the name and the
  waitlist, not chase demand we cannot serve.
- **Emotional centre:** from "I drop him off and hope someone is paying attention" to "he's in safe hands; someone is
  watching with me". The proof is proactive contact: the teacher reaches the parent before the parent notices.
- **Busy-parent paradox:** if most parents do not follow up, a dashboard full of data is not the selling point (it is
  more work for them). The selling point is "you don't have to chase anyone: we come to you". The dashboard is proof,
  not the promise.
- "Elite teachers" is an unprovable claim with two teachers; show the selection and the behaviour instead of saying it.
- **Young adults' emotional centre** (to test): from "I feel far, and I'll be judged if I come back" to "someone my
  age gets me and walks with me, no lectures". Same brand line, different scene.
- **Audience split** to test: ~60% parents / ~40% young adults for the first 4 weeks, then follow the numbers
  (waitlist by track: "me" vs "my child"). Every piece still speaks to one of them.
- A higher rate for learners who ask for the owner by name could undercut "every teacher works the same way" if it is
  public; keep it unlisted if it ever happens.

## Experiments
1. **VOC round 1 [Approved 2026-11-12, v2 messages; owner sends]:** WhatsApp questions to past families the owner is still in touch with (no
   call). Parents (mostly mothers) and students; under-16s through the parent, never a direct message to a minor
   (content RULES §4.4). Sent Saturday ~10–11 am their time, personalised, one reminder after a week at most. No offer
   or link in the message. Answers are research only: nothing is quoted publicly without written permission.
   Before sending: the owner confirms no non-solicitation clause with a former academy.

   v2 (owner's notes 2026-11-12): personal and casual, not professional; short; a check-in first (no contact for ~6
   weeks since he left the institute); about the deen in general, not "a Quran academy"; no "how did you find a
   teacher" (the institute assigned the teacher). Sent in two steps: the check-in, then the questions after they reply.

   **Parent, step 1**
   > Assalamu alaikum [Name]! It's Mahmoud 😊 It's been a while. How are you all? How's [child] doing?

   **Parent, step 2** (after the reply)
   > Alhamdulillah, so good to hear! Can I ask you something quick? I'm working on something of my own for teaching
   > kids the deen, and I'd really value your honest take. A voice note is totally fine:
   > 1. When [child] started, what worried you most about online classes?
   > 2. Before the first class, did you know anything about who would teach [child]? What would you have wanted to know?
   > 3. Honestly, what made you stay with me? 😄
   > 4. Anything that annoyed you with other teachers?

   **Student 16+, step 1**
   > Hey [Name]! It's Ustadh Mahmoud 😄 Long time! How've you been?

   **Student 16+, step 2**
   > Glad to hear it! Random question: I'm building something for learning the deen and I want it to NOT be boring 😅
   > Quick answers are fine:
   > 1. What made our classes worth it for you?
   > 2. What's the most boring thing a teacher can do?
   > 3. Your dream class: what's in it?

   **Under 16:** to the parent after step 2: "And could you ask [child] these three for me? 😄" + the student questions.

   What each question is for: worries = the "before" emotion; knowing the teacher = tests our "choose your own
   teacher" edge; why they stayed = the real differentiator; what annoyed them = the enemy we stand against.
