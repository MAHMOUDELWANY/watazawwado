# Audit: Zad Essentials Kids and Teens, Lessons 1–3, against the motion-stage standard (2026-10-24)

Measured against `.claude/skills/curriculum-builder/owner-feedback.md` and the approved reference (Adults Lesson 1).
Page numbers count from 0, as in the preview tool (`p=`). Tools: `scripts/curriculum/audit.ts` (build shape and 5-word
repeats per page) and `scripts/curriculum/dump.ts` (page text).

## Across all six lessons
1. **Text order (fails everywhere).** The Kids/Teens player shows the lines *before* the ayah or hadith (text-first is
   only switched on for Adults). So every evidence page tells the hadith in our words, then the hadith says it again:
   the exact rejection "you make the reader read it twice". Fix: text first for every version, then rewrite the lines
   so they only add something new.
2. **Thin pages.** 59 of the 85 pages that are not a cover or a game have 1–2 lines (or none). Standard: 3–5 lines per idea
   page, each a step of the explanation.
3. **English first.** Evidence is shown in the painting/manuscript layouts with the Arabic as the lead. Standard: English
   meaning as the hero, Arabic beneath, reference under it (the motion stage `Quote` does this).
4. **Motion.** Every idea page is a painting with a slow drift or a small code overlay; nothing moves to explain the idea.
   Standard: one graphic per page on a full 1600 × 900 stage, built step by step. Kids keep their library characters
   (Yusuf, Maryam, Jiddo, Mishmish; Zayn, Noor, Ethan… for Teens) as the people on the stage.
5. **Questions over pictures.** The "Think first" card sits over the painting. Standard: question in the left column
   under the lines; big answer cards on the right with the graphic stepped back.
6. **Status.** Kids L1–3 are `reviewed: true` and open to booked students. Any text change there needs the owner's new
   approval: ask him whether the improved versions replace the approved ones at once or wait as drafts.

## Kids L1 · The Visitor in White (16 pages)
- Driving question works ("why ask what you already know?" from Ms. Clarke, answered by the reveal). Keep it.
- p3 One day in Madinah: 3 lines retell the hadith ("very white… very black") before it appears. Rewrite as what to
  notice (no dust? neat? who walks in like that?).
- p4 Something does not add up: lines say the hadith's two facts in other words. Lines should add the reasoning (a
  desert journey = dust; a small town = everyone known; both cannot be true).
- p5 How he sat: lines repeat "knee to knee", "hands on thighs". Add what it shows (he came to learn; adab).
- p6 Islam: 1 line. Needs the "what holds a Muslim's life up?" step and what each pillar is, in kids' words.
- p7 "You have spoken the truth": 2 lines, restate. Add the link back to Ms. Clarke as the turn of the story.
- p8 Iman: 1 line; p9 Ihsan: line 2 paraphrases the hadith.
- p10 Do you know who he was?: lines repeat the hadith word for word (test: "umar do you know who").
- p11 It was Jibril!: lines repeat the hadith. Add: no dust, nobody knew him, he knew the answers → all explained.
- p12 Three doors: fine as a summary; 2 lines.

## Kids L3 · Allah Is One (16 pages)
- Driving question works (two captains → one order → one Lord).
- p5 If there were two: 1 line ("The Quran makes this very point"); needs the step from the boat to the ayah.
- p6 La ilaha illallah: 2 lines, no explanation of the two halves on screen.
- p7 Only to Him, p9 Four things about Allah: 1–2 lines; good items.
- p8 Say: He is Allah, One: 2 lines, no explanation (the next page carries it).
- p11 Allah loves him: the lines tell the whole story before the hadith; the hadith then arrives as an end line. Show the
  story as context (context-first) so the hadith is the payoff, not a repeat.
