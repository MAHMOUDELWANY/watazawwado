# Egyptian Arabic · binding rules (all versions)

Design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-25-subjects-curricula-design.md` §4.6 and §5. `docs/curriculum/RULES.md` and the
design win on conflict.

1. **Two scripts, one scope.** Arabic-script track (Kids / Teens / Adults, Adults first) and a Franco track (one version,
   adults) that reuses the same lessons with every Arabic line in the owner's Franco spelling. Learners of the Arabic
   track already read (Literacy A first).
2. **Scope only from books:** *Kallimni 'Arabi Bishweesh* (beginner) → *Kallimni 'Arabi* → *Aktar* → *Mazboot* → *fi Kull
   Haaga*; *Egyptian Colloquial* for the Fusha → Egyptian sound shifts. We take the order of functions, structures and
   vocabulary fields; never their dialogues, drills, pictures or sentences.
3. **Real Cairo, real people.** Our learners are English-speaking Muslims in the West: in-laws in Egypt, a trip, study in
   Cairo, an Egyptian friend or colleague. Scenes are everyday Cairo (the building, the doorman, the bakery, the taxi,
   the family visit), told with faceless people. Islamic greetings are part of the language; teaching religion is not the
   subject.
4. **Comprehensible input first:** every lesson opens with a short scene the learner follows from the pictures and motion
   before any rule. Then one structure, then the phrases, then practice, then a role-play.
5. **One structure per lesson,** shown as a pattern the learner can reuse (اسم + ي / ـَك / ـِك). Grammar words stay in
   English and stay few.
6. **Egyptian spelling:** written as Egyptians write it (إيه، فين، إزّيك), not Fusha. Short vowels only where they carry
   meaning (اسمَك / اسمِك) or help a first reading. Every Arabic line has a light transliteration in `vocab` for the first
   five lessons (the books withdraw it later; so do we).
7. **Audio:** every Arabic line plays on tap in the Mahmoud Egyptian clone (`eg`), with its emotion. The English lines are
   the learner's language: short, warm, in our voice (`humanizer`).
8. **Teacher-proof and adult:** warm-up, input, practice, role-play prompts and the mission are all in the lesson. Adults
   lessons have no optional pages; the review is a set of real moments with the reason after each answer.
9. Every new word or phrase goes in the page's `vocab`: it becomes the learner's spaced-repetition deck.
