**One question:** how do small words make an argument stronger, and what do they do to the endings?

| Version | Scene | Pages | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kids (`fusha-kids-3`) | the trial of Mishmish: Maryam defends, Yusuf accuses, Jiddo judges | 13 | إِنَّ (for sure) and لَكِنَّ (but); the first word goes to ـَ / ـًا, the second stays; إِنَّهُ، لَكِنَّهُ; "true thing, but…" |
| 12+ (`fusha-12-3`) | Friday debate club: should phones be banned at school? | 13 | all six sisters with their moods (إِنَّ، أَنَّ، لَكِنَّ، كَأَنَّ، لَيْتَ، لَعَلَّ), ٱسْمُهَا ـَ / خَبَرُهَا ـُ, attached pronouns, one sentence through six moods |

Device (`g-fx3.tsx`): the seesaw. A noun sentence sits on a plank; when a sister lands on the first seat it goes down
and that word swaps to its ـَ form (gold), while the predicate stays up in ـُ (teal). The 12+ seesaw page promises
كَانَ as its mirror in Lesson 8.
