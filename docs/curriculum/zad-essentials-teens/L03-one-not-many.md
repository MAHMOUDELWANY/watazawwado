# Lesson 3: One, Not Many

Zad Essentials · Teens (12–17) · Part 1 · ~45 min

Waiting for the owner's review and images. Pages live only in `src/curriculum/content/zad-teens/l03-one-not-many.ts`.

## Goal

The learner understands لا إله إلا الله: there can only be one God, and only He deserves worship. They see that the
"gods" of their own world (likes, money, fitting in, their own desires) can quietly take that place.

## Outcomes

1. Explains with reasons why there can't be two or a team of all-powerful gods (two wills, Al-Anbiya 21:22).
2. Gives the two halves of the shahada (no one deserves worship / except Allah) and what each does.
3. Names "modern gods" from their own world and links them to Al-Jathiyah 45:23.
4. Knows what al-Ikhlas says about Allah (One, as-Samad, no child or parent, none equal) and why it equals a third of the
   Quran in reward (Bukhari 5013).
5. Sorts at least 7 of 8 statements correctly in the challenge.

## Spark

Recall of Lesson 2 (what begins has a cause; not from nothing, not by itself; how is not who) and "How did your challenge
go?". Then the Lesson 2 cliffhanger: Ethan's game has a whole team of gods. "Why one?"

## Rational steps (before the text)

1. **Two bosses.** Two all-powerful gods disagree (one wants the sun to rise, the other doesn't). Either one wins, and the
   loser wasn't all-powerful, or nothing happens, and neither was. A God who can be blocked isn't God.
2. **A team that always agrees.** Then who decides? If one decides and the others follow, he is the real God and the
   others are not. If no one decides, there is no order at all.
3. **The universe shows one hand.** The same laws hold across everything we can observe. That is what one will looks like.
4. **The shahada.** It first says no (to every false god), then yes (to Allah). You can't fill the place until you empty it.
5. **Modern gods.** Nobody at school bows to a statue, but anything you obey over Allah takes His place: "his desire as
   his god" (45:23).
