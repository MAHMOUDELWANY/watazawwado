# Lesson 1: The Stranger with No Dust

Zad Essentials · Teens (12–17) · Part 1 · ~45 min
Status: **brief, waiting for the owner's review** (2026-10-21). Pages, their order and text will live only in the code
(`src/curriculum/content/zad-teens/l01-the-stranger-with-no-dust.ts`, RULES §1.12).

## Goal

The learner sees that the deen has three levels (Islam, Iman, Ihsan) and that it is something you **learn and
understand**, not only something you inherit. This opens the whole course: "Do I believe it, or was I just told?"

## Outcomes (checked mostly through the challenge)

1. Retells the hadith of Jibril in a few sentences: a stranger with no sign of travel, five questions (three of them
   the levels of the deen), the reveal that it was Jibril, "he came to teach you your deen".
2. Explains the three levels in their own words: **Islam** (what I do), **Iman** (what I believe), **Ihsan** (doing
   it as if I see Allah, knowing He sees me).
3. Sorts at least 7 of 8 real-life deeds and beliefs into the right door, against the clock.
4. Can say why Islam asks for understanding, not blind following: the Quran itself criticises "we just follow what
   our fathers did" (2:170), and Jibril came *to teach*.
5. Ahd: picks one question about Islam they have never asked out loud and writes it on their own Why List.

## Spark (the hook)

Zayn's phone at lunch. Ethan, half joking: "Be honest. If you'd been born in my family, would you even be Muslim?"
Zayn types, deletes, types again. Sends nothing. He opens a new note and writes the first line of the **Why List**:
"Am I Muslim only because my parents are?" The learner answers first: "What would you have replied?"

## Rational step (before the text)

1. **Why would someone ask questions he already knows the answers to?** A teacher who asks the class something she
   knows is teaching the people listening. The stranger in the hadith asks and then says "you're right", which surprises
   the Sahaba: that only makes sense if he already knew.
2. **Inheriting is a start, not the finish.** Everyone starts with what their family gives them: language, food,
   faith. The real question is whether you have looked at it yourself. A teen who knows *why* holds it differently
   from one who only knows *that*. (The full answer to "Why Islam?" comes in Lesson 11; the cliffhanger points there.)

- the answer on Islam (the five pillars) and the answer on Iman (the six articles);
- «فعَجِبْنا له يَسأَلُه ويُصَدِّقُه» (the Sahaba's surprise), the heart of the rational step.

**Tafsir for 2:170.** Al-Sa'di (quran.com/2:170/tafsirs/91, read 2026-10-21): they were content with imitating their
fathers and turned away from the messengers; it is a weak excuse for rejecting the truth; whoever makes the truth his
aim and weighs it fairly against other claims will see it and follow it. Our use: Islam asks you to know, not just copy.
Taught carefully: **it is not about our parents being wrong**; the ayah is about following *without understanding*.
Holding the same faith as your parents is good; holding it with knowledge makes it yours.

**Tadabbur stop (2:170), Teens depth: the root and how it shapes the meaning.** Candidate word: يَعْقِلُونَ, root
ع ق ل. The sense that *'aql* holds a person back from harm, as a rope (*'iqal*) holds a camel, is common in the
dictionaries but **needs review**: confirm it in a recognised source (morphology through the connector; the meaning
through a tafsir on quran.com) before it goes on a page. Question: "Do I use my mind on my deen, or just my habit?"
Action: ask one real question this week (the Ahd).

## Not in this lesson (Claude's calls)

- **The signs of the Hour** in Muslim 8 are allowed for Teens but left for Lesson 8 (the Last Day), where they fit, so
  Lesson 1 stays on one idea.
- No doubt is pushed further than Ethan's question. The lesson raises "inherited faith" honestly and promises the full
  answer in Lesson 11; it does not argue it out here.

## Visual and motion notes

- Opening: the school canteen at lunch, Zayn's phone glowing, the typing dots that appear and vanish (code-drawn).
- The hadith scene: Madinah, the masjid, the circle of sitting places. The Prophet ﷺ and the Sahaba are never drawn;
  Jibril only as the white-clothed stranger from behind at a distance. **Wow moment (motion):** the camera pushes in on
  the stranger's clean white clothes while sand blows across the frame and none of it lands on him, then the line "no
  sign of travel on him" appears.
- The three doors return from the Kids series, redrawn in the anime look, and open one by one.
- The Why List (a phone note, code-drawn) appears for the first time; it becomes the series device.
- Sound effects only: a notification ping, wind and sand, a door opening, a soft chime on the reveal.

## Challenge (Athar)

**"Three Doors, 60 Seconds":** eight cards fly in (praying Fajr, believing in the angels, giving charity in secret,
fasting Ramadan, believing in Qadar, being honest when no one would know, Hajj, believing in the Last Day). The learner
drops each into Islam, Iman or Ihsan. Beat your own time on a second try. No points saved, no ranking.

## Ahd

The learner picks one: (a) write one honest question about Islam on their own Why List and bring it next lesson;
(b) ask a parent or the teacher one question they have never asked out loud; (c) when something in salah feels like
habit, pause once and remember "He sees me".
