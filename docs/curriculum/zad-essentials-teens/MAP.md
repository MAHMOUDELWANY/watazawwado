# Zad Essentials · Teens (12–17): Unit Map

Status: **draft, waiting for the owner's review** (2026-10-21). Rules: `../RULES.md` (§3 Teens column) and `../PLAN.md`
§3a. Fiqh: Shafi'i only, no differences shown. Each lesson is about 45 minutes and split into pages, so the teacher can
finish in 30 or stretch to 60. Evidence marked ✔ was already verified for the Kids lessons (quran.com connector, Dorar)
and is reused without re-research. Everything else is "to verify" before its lesson brief.

## Why this version exists (owner, 2026-10-21)

Most Muslim teens in the West don't know the basics of their deen, and many are pushed into worship without anyone
telling them what it is or why. The deen then feels like a cage, not a conviction. This version roots them in the
essentials (ما لا يسع المسلم جهله) **by conviction and knowledge, in a teen's own style**.

The owner's tone rules:
- **No direct preaching, no heavy fear.** Jannah and the Fire are both named honestly (RULES §1.11), never used as a threat.
- **Story and real-life situations**, concrete and believable. The teen learns without noticing and enjoys it.
- **Burning curiosity**: the learner talks, asks and argues back, not just listens.
- **One connected story** across all lessons, like the Kids series.

What follows from that (Claude's calls):
- **"Why" before "what" in every lesson:** the situation, then the reasoning, then the evidence, then the ruling. The
  ruling is said clearly, but only once the learner has seen the wisdom behind it.
- **The teen answers first.** Every Spark ends with "What would *you* say?" before anything is explained.
- **No word against parents.** The lessons give the teen the understanding that was missing; they never say "your
  parents got it wrong". The teacher can pass a note to the family in the lesson report.
- **The Ahd is chosen by the learner** from two or three options, a personal challenge, never an order.

## The story (series world) [Proposed]

- **Zayn (15)** and **Noor (13)**, brother and sister, at a secondary school in a Western city. Muslims by family, but
  until now they "just did it because we were told". Their arc across the course: from *because Mum said so* to *because
  I know and I'm convinced*.
- **The Why List:** a shared note on their phones where they write every question they couldn't answer (from a
  friend, a video, a comment, their own head). Each lesson crosses one question off and ends with a new one landing on
  the list, which is the cliffhanger. The last lesson shows the full list, every line crossed off. (The Teens version of
  the Kids' Zad backpack.)
- **Ethan**, Zayn's non-Muslim friend: curious and fair, asks honest questions ("Why don't you eat pork?", "Do you really
  believe that?"). Never a villain.
- **Amira**, Noor's friend: was made to pray and wear hijab with no explanation, and quietly stopped. Her arc mirrors the
  problem this version exists to solve: by the end she prays by choice. Handled gently, never mocked.
- **Hamza**, their older cousin (about 22, a university student), whom they message when they get stuck. He doesn't
  lecture; he asks them questions back until they reach the answer. For the girls' puberty lesson Noor talks to **Aunt
  Salma** instead.
- Settings: school corridor and canteen, the bus, a group chat, a comment section, the mosque youth room, home at
  night, a football pitch, a phone screen.
- No prophets, Sahaba or Jibril are shown (RULES §1.10).

## How a Teens lesson feels (on top of the four stations)

- **Spark:** a phone screen, a DM, a group chat, a video comment or a moment at school. From Lesson 2 on: a quick recall
  and "How did your challenge go?"
- **Zad:** the reasoning built step by step with the learner, "If you were Zayn, how would you answer?", then the
  evidence. Doubts are answered in the lesson they belong to: reasoning first, then text, never planting a doubt the
  learner didn't have (RULES §3).
- **Athar:** tadabbur stop on every ayah. For Teens that means the key word's **root and how it shapes the meaning**.
  Then the **challenge game**.
- **Ahd:** a personal challenge the learner picks. Then the new question on the Why List.

## Challenge game (end of every lesson)

A short game (3–5 min) that checks the lesson's outcomes quietly and feeds the teacher's report. It is a challenge, not
a children's game: beat your own time, or argue your way through. Types we rotate:
- **Reply in 30 seconds:** a DM arrives, pick or build the best reply.
- **Spot the flaw:** find the weak link in an argument or a viral claim.
- **Myth or fact:** swipe.
- **Build the argument:** put the steps of the reasoning in order.
- **Case file:** a real-life fiqh scenario, give the ruling (Shafi'i).
- **Odd one out**, **fill the gap**, **match pairs**.

Ethics guard (RULES §1.11): no points for worship, no streaks, no leaderboard against other students.

## Differences from the Kids map (Claude's calls)

- Part 1 grows from 10 to 11 lessons: Lesson 5 (the Quran's challenge) is new, and "Why am I here?" now also answers
  "Why Islam?", which is the heart of this version.
- Part 2 adds three lessons on puberty and ghusl (16, 17, 18a/18b), per the owner's decision of 2026-10-21. Najasah and
  istinja stay one lesson.
- Part 3 adds making up missed prayers (30) and fitting prayer around the school day (23).
- Part 5 adds hijab and boundaries (37) and friends (38); "Little Sunnahs" (Kids 35) folds into 36 and 39.
- Total: 40 lessons (41 lesson files; a learner takes 40).
