# Zad Essentials · Adults (18+): binding rules

Owner decisions for the Adults version (2026-10-22). They sit on top of the general rules in `../RULES.md` (which win where
the two disagree, except for the points below that the owner changed for this version) and `../../../RULES.md` (platform).
The lessons are in `MAP.md`; the look will be in `../style/adults.md` (made before any image). Procedure: the
`curriculum-builder` skill.

## 1. Audience and aim
- A Muslim adult (20s–50s) with a background: grew up Muslim or among Muslims, has heard khutbahs, "knows most things".
  **New Muslims are not the audience**; a separate course may come later if there is demand.
- The aim is **yaqeen**: knowledge that becomes certainty and moves the learner, as the Companions' certainty of Jannah
  moved them. With it, **pride in the deen and identity**: unafraid to say "I am Muslim".

## 2. Lesson length (changes `../RULES.md` §1.7 for this version)
- **Every lesson is 60 minutes.**
- **No optional pages.** Every page is shown, in order, however many there are. No `optional: true` in Adults lessons.

## 3. Content
- **Detailed and rich**, far more than Kids and Teens, but **never information for its own sake**: each fact serves the
  conviction or the change.
- **Projections onto real life:** quotes and moments from well-known people, matched to age (influencers, TikTokers and
  creators for the 20s; business, sport and public figures for the 30s–50s), so the learner feels the point from inside.
  Every quote comes from a public, checkable source (interview, podcast, book, post) and is recorded in `../sources.md`.
  No mockery of anyone, nothing that promotes what is haram.
- **Role models only from the Companions, Islamic history, and recently deceased Muslims of sound character.** Living
  people appear only as a quote that shows an idea, never as someone to follow. The owner approves each recent name.
- **Connected lessons:** the learner's notebook **Zad al-Yaqeen (زاد اليقين)**, Then and Now, and the hand-off question (see `MAP.md`).
- Fiqh: Shafi'i; where a recognised difference exists, one line says so and sends the learner to the teacher.
- Doubts answered in depth inside their lesson, reasoning first, never planting one the learner didn't have.
- Tadabbur on every ayah: key word, root, context and recognised tafsir notes.
- Check: case questions and a short review. Ahd: a habit or decision the learner chooses and writes in Zad al-Yaqeen.
- **Money (Lesson 44):** any case named is defined with its ruling, reason and halal way out, so the teacher can explain
  it; a recognised minority fatwa is named with its source and condition in one line.
- **Lesson 20 is two parallel lessons:** 20a for women, 20b for men (how men treat their mothers, sisters and wives, and
  how the Messenger ﷺ treated his wives). The teacher picks by learner.

## 4. Voice
- **More advanced English** than the Kids and Teens versions: the register of an educated adult, still clear and never
  academic for show. Ayat and hadith meanings faithful and fuller, in plain English.
- Written the owner's way (`mahmoud-voice`): the learner is asked before being told, warm and honest, one wise line per
  lesson. The academy speaks, not one person.
- `humanizer` on every line so nothing reads as AI-written.

## 5. Look and slides
- Calm, cinematic, editorial (`../RULES.md` §3), designed for a 20- to 50-year-old mind. No recurring characters, so no
  series opening.
- The premium bar and slide rules of `../RULES.md` §1.10 apply in full: rich motion, a scene build on every idea page, one
  wow motion scene per lesson, text that fits, no thin slides, no lesson length on covers, the Finish card.
- **The text first, then what we draw from it** (owner, 2026-10-23). An ayah or hadith is never paraphrased in the slide
  lines before it appears; the learner would read the same thing twice. The page shows the text, then one or two lines
  that add something the text does not say: what it means here, the question it raises, the link to the next page.
  Lines may come first only when they set the scene the text needs (`contextFirst`, e.g. Hanzalah's story before the
  Prophet's ﷺ reply), and even then they never restate it. The player enforces the order for `version: 'adults'`.
