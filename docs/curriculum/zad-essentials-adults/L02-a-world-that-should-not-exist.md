## The question that drives the lesson

"Why is there anything at all, rather than nothing?" Lesson 1 ended on "if certainty is the goal, where does it start?";
this lesson answers: with the first question of all, is there a Maker. The learner closes two of the three possible doors
by reasoning, then finds that At-Tur 52:35 names the same two doors in the same order (the wow), and that the very next
ayah (52:36) diagnoses the real illness as a lack of *yaqeen*, not a lack of proof, which ties the lesson to the course.

## Goal

The learner can show, reason first and then from the Quran, why a universe that began needs a Maker outside it, and can
answer "science has replaced God" and "then who made God?" calmly and in their own words.

## Outcomes

1. States the rule "whatever begins to exist has a cause" and gives everyday examples.
2. Knows that scientists trace the universe to a beginning (about 13.8 billion years ago, NASA) and why that sharpens the
   question instead of removing it.
3. Gives the three possibilities (from nothing, by itself, by another) and why the first two fail.
4. Recognises the same argument in At-Tur 52:35 and the diagnosis of 52:36 ("they have no certainty").
5. Takes apart Hawking's sentence ("a law such as gravity… create itself from nothing"), explains "how is not who", and
   answers "who made God?" with Al-Hadid 57:3.
6. Writes the second line of Zad al-Yaqeen and chooses one habit.

## Rational steps (before the text)

1. **Nothing starts itself.** A phone, a crack in the wall, a fever, a rumour: when something begins we look for its
   cause. Every science, court and doctor works on this.
2. **The universe began.** NASA Science, "Universe: Overview": around 13.8 billion years ago (already logged for Teens
   L2). We never say an ayah "predicted" the Big Bang (RULES §1.2).
3. **Three doors, no fourth.** From nothing; by itself; by another. Nothing has no power, no laws and no potential (space,
   a vacuum, energy fields and laws are all *something*). Making yourself means existing before you exist.
4. **How is not who.** Science explains how; knowing how a printer puts ink on paper does not remove the author.
5. **Who made God?** The rule covers what *begins*. A line of borrowers, each passing on a pound they never owned, never
   delivers a pound: borrowed existence needs One who has it without borrowing. Allah is al-Awwal (57:3).
6. **A world that can be read.** The laws hold everywhere we can see and can be written in mathematics; Einstein called
   that comprehensibility "the eternal mystery of the world". Order and a mind that reads it point to a will.

**Tafsir (quran.com connector, read 2026-10-25).**
- **52:35** al-Sa'di (quran.com/52:35/tafsirs/91): reason and revelation agree it is one of three; created from nothing
  with no creator "هذا عين المحال", or they created themselves, also impossible; so the third is settled: Allah created
  them, and He alone deserves worship. Ibn Kathir (quran.com/52:35/tafsirs/169): neither is true; Allah brought them into
  existence after they were nothing; cites Jubayr's hadith and notes he came after Badr to ransom the captives while still
  an idolator, and this was one of the reasons he later accepted Islam.
- **52:36** al-Sa'di: they have no complete knowledge and certainty that would let them benefit from the proofs. Ibn
  Kathir: they knew Allah alone is the Creator, and fell into idolatry because they had no firm belief.
- **57:3** al-Sa'di (logged in Teens L2): al-Awwal is the One with nothing before Him; same words as Muslim 2713.
- **41:53** al-Sa'di (quran.com/41:53/tafsirs/91): signs in the horizons (the heavens and the earth, great events) and in
  themselves (the wonders of their own bodies) until it is clear beyond doubt. Ibn Kathir reports Mujahid, al-Hasan and
  as-Suddi on historical events, and adds the possible reading of man's own make-up. The lesson uses al-Sa'di's reading.
