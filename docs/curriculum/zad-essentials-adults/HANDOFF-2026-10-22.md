## How the decisions were reached (the conversation)
1. **Audience.** The owner first wanted one course for both a new Muslim and a knowledgeable Muslim. Claude proposed one
   version with two layers plus a short "First Steps" track for new Muslims. The owner then decided: **this course is for a
   Muslim adult with a background** (grew up among Muslims, heard khutbahs, knows most things); a course for new Muslims may
   come later if there is demand. So the course is detailed and rich, more than Kids and Teens.
2. **The problem it solves (owner's words, summarised).** Adult Muslims know, but are not *certain* (موقنين): call them to
   a battle and they would be afraid, unlike the Companions, who raced to martyrdom because they were sure of Jannah. The
   course must bring them to that **yaqeen**, and make them **proud of the deen and identity**, unafraid to say "I am Muslim".
3. **Length.** Every lesson is **60 minutes**. The owner rejected optional "go deeper" pages: **every page is shown, in
   order, however many there are**.
4. **Projections (إسقاطات).** Real-life examples and quotes from famous people matched to age: influencers, TikTokers and
   bloggers for the 20s; figures that suit people in their 30s–50s. The learner should feel the point from inside and be
   convinced. Claude's rule, accepted: every quote from a public, checkable source, recorded in `../sources.md`; no mockery.
5. **Role models.** Asked whether living Muslim celebrities can be positive examples (risk: they change or a scandal
   breaks), the owner said: **only the Companions, history, and Muslims who died recently and were of sound character**.
   Living people only as a quote that illustrates an idea. The owner approves each recent name.
6. **Not an information dump.** Advanced English, more than the other series; written the owner's way (`mahmoud-voice`:
   ask before telling, warm, honest, one wise line); `humanizer` on every line so it never reads as AI.
7. **Connection between lessons is the most important thing**, and the design must speak to minds aged 20 to 50+.
   Claude's answer, approved: the learner's notebook, Then and Now (a Companion or history moment beside a moment from
   today), and a hand-off question at the end of each lesson that the next one opens with.
8. **Answers to the map's open points:**
   - 46 lessons: fine.
   - The notebook was first called "Certainty Notebook"; the owner wanted a name close to the series name *Zad*, so it is
     **Zad al-Yaqeen (زاد اليقين)**. He may still rename it.
   - Money lesson (44): if a case is mentioned, it **must be defined** so the teacher can explain it. Six cases are listed
     in `MAP.md` (savings interest, credit cards, mortgage vs Islamic finance with the ECFR minority fatwa named, student
     loans, insurance, debt). All "to verify" in the brief.
   - Hayd and nifas: the owner chose **two parallel lessons**: 20a for women (their rulings, and how to carry themselves
     and deal with those around them as the deen asks) and 20b for men (how to treat their mothers, sisters and wives, and
     how the Messenger ﷺ treated his wives). Lesson 42 (Home) was adjusted so it does not repeat 20b.
