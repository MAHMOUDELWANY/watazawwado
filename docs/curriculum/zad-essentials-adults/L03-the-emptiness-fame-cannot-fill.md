## The question that drives the lesson

Lesson 2's hand-off: "If He exists, why do I not feel Him?" The lesson starts with people who had everything and said it
was not enough, names that hunger as the fitrah (Ar-Rum 30:30, Muslim 2658), shows what buries it (noise) and when it
breaks through (the storm at sea, 29:65; Ikrimah), answers "I just don't feel it" by reasoning, gives the hunger its
Quranic name (ma'ishah dank, 20:124) and ends on the food (13:28, the wow). Lesson 2 was the witness outside (the
horizons); Lesson 3 is the witness inside (41:53, "within themselves").

## Goal

The learner understands that the heart was made to know Allah, that nothing else fills that place, what covers the
fitrah and what lets it speak, and answers "I just don't feel it" honestly.

## Outcomes

1. Explains the argument from longing: every inborn hunger has something real that answers it; one hunger nothing in
   this world answers.
2. Defines fitrah from 30:30 and Muslim 2658 and can say why it is covered, never deleted.
3. Explains, from 29:65 and Ikrimah's story, why people call on Allah sincerely when the noise stops.
4. Answers "I don't feel Him" with reasons (the spoiled appetite, the heartbeat you don't feel).
5. Names the emptiness (20:124) and the cure (13:28; richness of the soul, Bukhari 6446).
6. Writes the third line of Zad al-Yaqeen and chooses one habit.

## Rational steps (before the text)

1. **The honest famous.** Two well-known men who reached the top and said it was not enough (quotes below).
2. **Every hunger points somewhere.** Thirst to water, hunger to food, loneliness to people; one longing that money,
   fame and success fail to answer points beyond this world.
3. **Covered, not deleted.** Noise (the next notification, goal, show, purchase) buries the longing; danger strips the
   noise away and it speaks.
4. **"I don't feel it."** Snack all afternoon and you don't feel hungry at dinner; you still need the meal. You don't
   feel your heartbeat now, and it has not stopped.

**Tafsir (quran.com connector, read 2026-10-25).**
- **30:30** al-Sa'di (quran.com/30:30/tafsirs/91): Allah placed in every heart love of the truth and preferring it, "وهذا
  حقيقة الفطرة"; whoever leaves it does so because something came over the fitrah and spoiled it, citing the hadith.
  Ibn Kathir (…/tafsirs/169): Allah made His creation to know Him and His oneness; Abu Hurayrah recited 30:30 after the
  hadith.
- **29:65** al-Sa'di (…/29:65/tafsirs/91): at sea, with the waves and fear of death, they leave their rivals and call on
  Allah alone; then on land they associate again; why not keep that sincerity in ease and hardship? Ibn Kathir: at times
  of calamity they call on Him alone, so why not always; cites Ikrimah's story (from Ibn Ishaq; the graded chain used on
  the slide is an-Nasa'i's).
- **20:124** Ibn Kathir (…/20:123/tafsirs/169): his life is hard in this world, with no tranquillity or ease of the chest;
  even if he seems comfortable, eats and wears what he likes and lives where he wants, his heart has no certainty and he
  is restless and in doubt. Al-Sa'di: some took it as the punishment of the grave; some as general, including the
  worries and pains of this life.
- **13:28** al-Sa'di (…/13:28/tafsirs/91): their anxiety and agitation leave and their joys come; it is fitting that hearts
  find rest in nothing but His remembrance; nothing is sweeter to hearts than loving their Creator and knowing Him; and if
  remembrance means His Book, hearts find rest only in certainty and knowledge.

## Then and Now

- **Then:** Ikrimah ibn Abi Jahl in the storm (an-Nasa'i 4067): when nothing but sincerity could save him at sea, he
  concluded nothing else saves on land, came back and accepted Islam. Not drawn: a ship on the waves and a line of light.
- **Now:** 1 a.m., the phone above your face; we scroll so we don't have to sit with the quiet where the fitrah speaks.

## Check (Athar)

Four cases (Sara, hollow after a good year; Adam, "I feel nothing in prayer"; the friend's "comfort blanket"; Yusuf, who
is never without input) and a review of nine moments in a row of three targets: Noise, The fitrah speaks, Remembrance.

## Ahd

Zad al-Yaqeen, third line, and one habit: (a) ten minutes a day with no input, ending with a short dhikr; (b) two
minutes of dhikr after one prayer a day, phone in another room; (c) each night, one line about a moment the heart reached
for Allah without being told.

## Hand-off

"The mind says: there is a Maker. The heart says: I was made for Him. So who is He? You cannot love someone you don't
know, and the sentence that names Him costs something to say." Lesson 4 (The Sentence That Costs Something).
