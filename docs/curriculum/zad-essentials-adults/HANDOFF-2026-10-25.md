## Points I am not sure of (the owner decides)

1. **Hawking as the spark of Lesson 2.** A famous non-Muslim scientist's sentence is the doubt the lesson takes apart. It
   is respectful and the learner dismantles it with the lesson's own tools, but it puts a well-known atheist voice early.
   Keep, or use an unnamed "a famous physicist wrote…"?
2. **Einstein's line** ("the eternal mystery of the world is its comprehensibility", 1936) for order and precision. He
   was not a believer in our sense; the teacher note says so. Keep?
3. **"Who made God?" with a line of borrowers** (each asks the next for a pound; if nobody owns one, none arrives). Teens
   used a train with no engine. Is the borrower picture clear enough for adults, or prefer the train?
4. **Tom Brady and Jim Carrey** as the two honest famous voices of Lesson 3 (one sport, one cinema; both living, not
   Muslim, quotes only). For learners in their 20s you may prefer a younger creator; I found no checkable quote I trusted
   overnight. Suggest one if you have it.
5. **Ikrimah's story.** The graded wording is an-Nasa'i 4067 (sahih per al-Albani). The context (he was among the few
   excluded from the amnesty at the conquest) is only in the teacher notes. Comfortable with the story in Lesson 3?
6. **The names of the review answers in Lesson 3:** "Noise / The fitrah speaks / Remembrance". Clear for adults?
7. **The example notebook lines** shown on the recall pages ("knowing is not yet seeing", "the world did not make
   itself") are samples, since the real line is each learner's own. Fine as samples?
8. **Ta-Ha 20:124 in a lesson about feelings.** Al-Sa'di gives two readings (the grave; or the worries of this life too);
   the slide uses Ibn Kathir's wording about this life and the note gives both. Agree?
9. **Length.** 25 and 24 pages at 60 minutes, every page shown, like Lesson 1 (26 pages). If a lesson runs long in
   practice, the cases are the natural place to trim.
