# Lesson 2 — Who Made It All?

## Goal

The child leaves sure, by their own reasoning, that everything made has a Maker, and that this Maker is Allah. They
feel that their heart already knew it (fitrah), and they start noticing Allah's "clues" around them.

## Outcomes (what the teacher checks, mostly through the game)

1. Explains in their own words why a made thing cannot make itself (castle, drawing, phone).
2. Gives the three choices in At-Tur 52:35 (from nothing / made themselves / made by a Maker) and says why only the
   third one works.
3. Finds at least 3 of 4 "maker's clues" in a nature scene (the game).
4. Knows that the feeling "someone made this" is the fitrah Allah put in every person (Ar-Rum 30:30), and is proud that
   Islam matches what the heart already knows.
5. Behaviour: when something in creation amazes them, they remember who made it and thank Allah (Alhamdulillah).

## Rational step (before the text)

Maryam's castle (Lesson 1's cliffhanger). Three ways it could have got there:
1. It appeared from nothing. (Nobody believes that.)
2. The bricks clicked themselves together. (Bricks can't do anything.)
3. Someone built it. (Yes: Yusuf, at night, as a surprise.)

Then scale up: a castle of 200 bricks needs a builder, so what about a camel, the sky, or the child's own hand?
The "wow": At-Tur 52:35 asks the very same three questions, in the same order. The child worked out the ayah's argument
before seeing it. Al-Sa'di explains the ayah exactly this way: two choices are impossible, so the third (Allah created
them) is the only one left.

### Tafsir used (quran.com connector)

- **52:35** — al-Sa'di (quran.com/52:35/tafsirs/91): the three-way argument; two options impossible, so Allah is the
  Creator and alone deserves worship. Al-Muyassar (…/tafsirs/16): both options are "باطل ومستحيل". Al-Wasit (Tantawi,
  …/tafsirs/93): a rebuke for not thinking about how they themselves were made.
- **30:30** — al-Wasit (…/tafsirs/93): fitrah originally means "how a thing was first made" (الخلقة); people are made
  ready to accept the true deen; quotes the Bukhari hadith. Al-Sa'di (…/tafsirs/91): Allah put love of the truth in every
  heart; whoever leaves it does so because something spoiled it.
- **88:17–20** — al-Sa'di and al-Muyassar: an invitation to look at the camel's wonderful making and how Allah made it
  serve people, the raised sky, the firm mountains, the spread-out earth.

### Science facts (the "look at the camel" page)

From San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance, "Camel" (animals.sandiegozoo.org/animals/camel), checked 2026-10-18: a clear
inner eyelid that shields the eye in sandstorms while it still sees; a double row of extra-long eyelashes; nostrils it
can close against sand; wide feet that spread on sand; a week or more without water, then up to 145 litres in one go.
We state these as facts about the camel and say "look how it was made". We never claim the ayah "predicted" them
(RULES §1.2).

## Station plan (no page text here)

- **Spark:** quick recall of the three doors; check last week's Ahd ("what question did you ask your mum or dad?"); then
  the LEGO castle: "Who built it?"
- **Zad:** the three choices for the castle (the child answers before each one is shown) → scale up to bigger things →
  the wow: 52:35 asks the same three questions → fitrah: babies everywhere are born ready to know Allah (30:30 + the
  hadith's first clause).
- **Athar:** tadabbur stops; "look at the camel" (88:17–20); the game: find the maker's clues in a nature scene.
- **Ahd:** the Clue Hunt: this week, with the family, find 3 clues (a leaf, a bird, the night sky, your own eyes…), the
  child picks which, and say Alhamdulillah for each. Cliffhanger to Lesson 3.

## Age-band check (RULES §3, Kids)

- Characters: Yusuf, Maryam, Jiddo, Mishmish (the cat could knock the castle over: a made thing breaks by itself, it
  never builds itself).
- No doubts raised: we do not present "some people say it made itself" as a view to answer. The three choices are a
  thinking game, not a debate.
- Identity: Muslims are told by Allah to look and think (88:17); our deen agrees with the heart and the mind.
