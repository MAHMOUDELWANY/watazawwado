## Goal

The child understands لا إله إلا الله: Allah is One, nobody shares in running the world, and nobody else deserves our
worship. They feel it is true with their own mind (two captains spin the boat), meet the Quran's own proof (21:22), and
learn Surat al-Ikhlas as the way Allah describes Himself.

## Outcomes (what the teacher checks, mostly through the game)

1. Explains with the boat (or any "two bosses" example) why the world could not run in perfect order with two gods.
2. Says what لا إله إلا الله means in simple words: nobody deserves to be worshipped except Allah.
3. Gives the four things Surat al-Ikhlas tells us about Allah (One; everyone needs Him and He needs no one; He has no
   parents and no children; nothing is like Him).
4. Marks at least 5 of 6 sentences about Allah as true or not true (the game).
5. Behaviour: turns to Allah first when they need help, and recites al-Ikhlas before sleep this week (the Ahd).

## Rational step (before the text)

The pedal boat from Lesson 2's cliffhanger: Yusuf steers left, Maryam steers right, the boat spins and goes nowhere. One
captain, and it goes straight to the shore. Then scale up: the sun rises every single morning, the moon keeps its path,
the seasons come in order. Everything works together, so it is run by One.
The "wow": Al-Anbiya 21:22 makes exactly this argument. Al-Sa'di (quran.com/21:22/tafsirs/91): the upper and lower world
are in perfect order with no clash, which shows their Planner is One; two planners would pull against each other, and the
order would break. Al-Muyassar (…/tafsirs/16): with gods besides Allah running them, their order would collapse.

### Tafsir used (quran.com connector)

- **21:22** — al-Sa'di (…/21:22/tafsirs/91): the "dalil al-tamanu'" (two wills block each other): if one wants a thing and
  the other does not, both cannot happen; if only one happens, the other is powerless; so the One whose will always
  happens is Allah. Al-Muyassar (…/tafsirs/16): their order would collapse (لاختلّ نظامهما).
- **47:19** — al-Muyassar (…/47:19/tafsirs/16): «لا معبود بحق إلا الله» (nobody is truly worshipped except Allah). Al-Sa'di
  (…/tafsirs/91): this knowledge is a duty on every person; among its paths: Allah alone creates and runs everything, Allah
  alone gives every blessing, and reflecting on His names and the signs in creation. We use the first two paths, which link
  back to Lesson 2.
- **112:1–4** — al-Sa'di (…/tafsirs/91): «قل» means say it firmly, believing it, knowing its meaning; أحد: alone in
  perfection, no equal; الصمد: the One all creation turns to for every need; لم يلد ولم يولد: from His perfect richness
  (He needs nothing); no one is equal to Him in names, attributes or actions. Al-Muyassar (…/tafsirs/16): الصمد = whom all
  creation turns to for their needs; 112:3 = no child, no parent, no wife; 112:4 = none of His creation is like Him.

## Owner decisions (2026-10-19)

The owner asked Claude to settle the five open points as an expert curriculum designer, within our rules (hook and
cliffhanger, 45-minute length, Kids band). Decided:

1. **112:3** stays in the surah (it is Quran and every child memorises it) and is taught plainly as one of the four facts,
   with no other religion mentioned. **The game card is changed** from "Allah has parents or children" to "Allah needs help
   from someone" (not true; from as-Samad). Reason: a stealth-assessment card should check understanding, not ask a child
   with Christian relatives to mark something about their family's belief as false; 112:3 is still checked in class by
   the "four pieces" recall.
2. **Hadith on screen:** only the Prophet's ﷺ answer «أخبِرُوه أنَّ اللهَ يُحِبُّه»; the teacher tells the story. One idea per
   page, few words on screen (RULES §1.11). Bukhari 5013 and 5017 stay in the teacher's notes.
3. **"Everyone needs Him" (as-Samad) stays as the optional page.** It adds a new idea (everything needs something, every
   chain ends at Allah) and a "did you know" (the word appears once in the Quran), so it is real 60-minute content, not
   filler (RULES §1.7). A 30-minute class skips it with no gap.
4. **Cliffhanger kept** (the book vs the coach). It plants Lesson 4's rational step from the map ("we learn best from
   someone who shows us") and ends on an open question answered next lesson.
5. **Ahd: al-Ikhlas only** (one small deed, today's surah, Kids band), worded honestly: the Prophet ﷺ recited it every
   night; children who know al-Falaq and an-Nas are invited to add them, as in Bukhari 5017.
