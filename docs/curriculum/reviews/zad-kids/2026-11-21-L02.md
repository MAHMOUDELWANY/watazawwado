### subject-scholar-1 · confirmed · status: open
- **where:** L2 p7 s0-s5 (all viewports; teacher notes EN + AR)
- **evidence:** Slide title "The same three questions"; line "Allah asked them in the Quran long ago, in the very same order."; reveal
"All three! From nothing? No. Made themselves? No. So Someone made them: Allah."; teacher note "the Quran asks the same questions,
in the same order" (AR: «القرآن بيسأل نفس الأسئلة وبنفس الترتيب»). Connector 52:35 = «أَمْ خُلِقُوا۟ مِنْ غَيْرِ شَىْءٍ أَمْ هُمُ ٱلْخَـٰلِقُونَ»:
two questions. Al-Sa'di (ar-saadi, quran.com/52:35/tafsirs/91), the scholar the lesson itself cites, lists three possibilities
but says the third is what remains once the two are refuted: «فإذا بطل هذان الأمران... تعين القسم الثالث أن الله الذي خلقهم».
Ibn Kathir (tafsirs/14): «لا هذا ولا هذا، بل الله هو الذي خلقهم». The stage on `steps\p07.png` (bottom-right tile) labels box 1

- **why:** A meaning stretched beyond the text. The ayah asks two questions and leaves the answer for the listener to supply; that is
its rhetorical force, and the tafsir the lesson cites says so. Telling a child "Allah asked the same three questions" puts
words in the Quran, and the "All three!" reveal answers a question the ayah did not ask.
- **fix:** Retitle p7 "The Quran's two questions". Line 2: "The Quran asks two of them, in the same order, and leaves the last answer
for you." Reveal: "Ways 1 and 2! From nothing? No. Made themselves? No. So only way 3 is left: Allah made them." Teacher note:
"the Quran asks Maryam's first two questions, in the same order; the answer it leaves is her third way." Drop the «الخالق» tag
on box 3 (show "Allah made them" in English only) so no third Arabic phrase looks quoted. Same correction in `l02-teacher-ar.ts`
and in the brief `L02-who-made-it-all.md` ("asks the very same three questions").
- **refuter:** Code p7 title, line 2, reveal "All three!" and explain all claim three questions in the same order, while 52:35 (connector) has two; sheet p07 last tile adds a third Arabic tag «الخالق» on card 3.

### age-band-3 · judgement · status: open
- **where:** L2 p10 s2–s5
- **evidence:** lines "Allah made the lock: your heart and your mind. And He made the key: Islam." / ask "Why do you think Islam
feels right in your heart?" (`steps/p10.png` tile 6); p8 tadabbur in data "Is my heart sure that Allah made everything?"
- **why:** The ask presupposes a feeling the child may not have; a 7-year-old who feels nothing particular, or a child from a
mixed or new-Muslim family, hears that something is missing in them. The two-place metaphor (lock = heart and mind, key
= Islam, fit = agreement) is relational analogy, which children under about 8 map unreliably without a worked example
(Gentner 1988, relational shift). Emotional safety rule for Kids: no guilt about inner states.
- **fix:** Ask: "The same Maker made the lock and the key. Who made your heart? Who sent Islam?" Reveal: "Allah made both.
That is why they fit." If the p8 tadabbur is later rendered (CW-3), change its question to "What clue makes my heart
sure today?"
- **refuter:** The p10 ask presupposes a feeling and the lock/key mapping is two-place; the reveal gives the child no way out if they feel nothing.
