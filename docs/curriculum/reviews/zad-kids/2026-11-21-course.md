## Verdict

**Accumulation does not work yet.** What the course has is a one-step chain. Each lesson opens on the previous lesson's
cliffhanger and asks about last week's mission, and that is all it carries forward. No slide in L2 or L3 names an earlier
lesson; the "Next time" pages name the following lesson three times, but no page ever names a lesson going back. By L3
nothing from L1 is on screen in words. The two series devices the MAP promised, the three doors and the filling backpack,
both fade: the doors become an unlabelled picture, and the L2 magnifier is gone from the bag in L3. The two sentences that
make the argument cumulative ("today we open the first door, Iman", "we know Allah made everything; how many makers?")
exist only in the teacher notes. The reasoning stack itself (doors → a Maker → the Maker is One) is right, and the
cliffhangers land. The fix is about 25 line edits, one asset, one component change and the web rule. No new pages.

### course-1 · major · confirmed
where: course (L2 p1, p2, p5; L3 p1, p2, p4–p13; all three games)
evidence: The ledger above. `grep -n "Lesson 1\|Lesson 2"` on the L2 and L3 lesson files: no hit in any `lines`, `prompt`
or `reveal`. The only forward names are the three "Next time" reveals ("Find out next time, in Lesson 3: Allah Is One").
L3 p2–p15 recall nothing from L1. The 18 game cards all come from their own lesson (L1 `cards` l.318–323, L2 l.371–376,
L3 l.366–371).
why: The owner's question is whether learning builds up. Memory turns into a web only when earlier material is pulled back
from memory in a new setting: retrieval practice (Roediger & Karpicke 2006), spacing (Cepeda et al. 2006), and anchoring new
material in what the learner already holds (Ausubel 1960). Here each lesson touches only the one before it, mostly by
retelling, so by L3 the child's first lesson has left the screen. Teens (course-1) and Adults (course-1) have the same
pattern, so this is the Zad build habit, not a Kids accident.
fix: Make the edits in "Smallest change set" below and adopt the Kids web rule (course-12). Minimum per lesson from L2:
two slide lines that name an earlier lesson and add something to it, one ask on a lesson two or more back, one game card
from an earlier lesson.

### course-2 · major · confirmed
where: course (L2 p1; L3 p1, p5, p6)
evidence: L2 p1 explain l.67: "Today we open the first door, Iman, with its very first step: knowing who made us." L3 p1
explain l.68: "Today we take the next step: we know Allah made everything. Now, how many makers are there?" The L3 brief
(Tafsir 47:19) says the paths "Allah alone creates and runs everything" were chosen to "link back to Lesson 2". On the
slides: L2 p1 reveal "Iman, Islam and Ihsan. Now, your mission…"; L3 p1 reveal "…Someone built it! Now tell me about your
Clue Hunt." The words "Maker" and "al-Khaliq" (taught on L2 p5 and p7, vocab chip `al-Khaliq` l.194) never appear in L3.
why: These two sentences are what makes the course a staircase: L2 is the first step behind the Iman door, and L3 asks how
many makers the L2 Maker is. Kept in the notes, they depend on a teacher who reads them aloud; on screen L3 looks like a new
topic about boats. A child who sees why today follows from last time can rebuild the chain alone (advance organiser,
Ausubel 1960). L3 curriculum-designer-3 covers the missing step inside L3; this is the step between lessons.
fix: L2 p1 reveal → "Iman, Islam and Ihsan. Today we open the first door, Iman. Its first step: Allah. Now, your mission:
what good question did you ask Mum or Dad?" L3 p1 reveal → "From nothing? No. Built itself? No. Someone built it: Allah,
al-Khaliq. Today's question: how many makers are there? But first, your Clue Hunt!" L3 p5 reveal → "The boat! Two bosses
and it spins. The world never spins out of control, so its Maker is One: Allah."

## The Kids web rule (for the curriculum-builder, every lesson from Lesson 2)

1. **Recall page, one template:** the three doors with labels, today's door lit and the current root chip ("Allah" for L2–L4).
   One closed-book ask on the last lesson and one on a lesson two or more back, answered before anything shows. The reveal
   names last week's mission and every option the child could pick.
2. **Bridge line:** the recall reveal says on the slide why today follows from last time ("We know Allah made everything.
   Today: how many makers?"). A bridge in `explain` alone does not count.
3. **Zad:** at least two slide lines name an earlier lesson by number ("Remember … from Lesson 1?") and add something new to it.
   A tool taught in one lesson (the three clues, yaqeen, the detective frame) is used again within the next two lessons.
4. **Game:** at least one card from an earlier lesson.
5. **Backpack:** every earlier provision stays visible in the bag; today's line names the last one.
6. **Cliffhanger:** stays inside the story, and its explain says which part of today's learning the next lesson completes.
7. **Terms:** the Zad glossary unchanged (the three doors, the three ways, the three clues, the Shahadah's parts, Ihsan's one
   gloss), no macrons on Kids slides. The brief's "Callbacks" table is filled before page text and checked by the course panel.
