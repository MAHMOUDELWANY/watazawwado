### curriculum-designer-1 · confirmed · status: open
- **where:** L2 p12 s0–s1 (desktop)
- **evidence:** `steps/p12.png` tile 2 shows the first card "Everything that begins has a cause"; lesson file p12 cards. Six of
the eight cards are the slide lines word for word or their direct negation: card 1 = p3 title; card 2 "The universe made
itself" = p5 item 2; card 5 "Something can come from absolutely nothing" = p5 item 1 reworded; card 6 "The universe had a
beginning" = p4 title; card 3/4 = p8 reveal; card 7 = p10 title question. The bins are only "Holds up / Flawed". The
brief's challenge is "Spot the Flaw", but nothing asks the learner to say *what* the flaw is.
- **why:** Outcomes 1–5 use production verbs (states, gives, explains, answers); the only recorded check is binary recognition

teacher, without being able to answer the boy at the back. Alignment principle (Biggs, constructive alignment, 1996): the
assessment task must call for the same performance as the outcome. Recognition is the weakest form of retrieval and
overestimates learning (Roediger & Karpicke 2006).
- **fix:** Rewrite the cards as disguised claims a teen meets online, each needing reasoning, and keep the "spot the flaw" verb:
"The Big Bang explains the universe, so it doesn't need a cause" (flawed: it began, so it needs one) · "Physics explains
how stars form, so no one made them" (flawed: how ≠ who) · "If everything needs a cause, God needs one too" (flawed: the
rule is about what begins) · "The universe could have popped out of nothing, no energy, no laws" (flawed) · "My phone
didn't make itself, so the universe probably didn't either" (holds) · "A thing can exist before it exists" (flawed) ·
"Explaining how a cake rises doesn't remove the baker" (holds) · "Something that began needs something before it" (holds).
Then add one final step to the game, or a page right after it: "Pick one flawed card and say the flaw out loud in one
sentence" (teacher marks it in the report). That is the performance outcomes 3–5 promise.
- **refuter:** Lesson file p12 cards are the slide lines or their negations in two bins, so the only scored check is recognition while the outcomes promise production.

### subject-scholar-1 · confirmed · status: open
- **where:** L2 p6 (At-Tur 52:35 meaning), p5 option 1 and p12 card 5 (course wording of the argument)
- **evidence:** Our meaning: "Were they made from nothing? Or did they make themselves?" p5 item "From nothing at all" is marked impossible; p12 card "Something can come from absolutely nothing" goes in Flawed. Al-Sa'di (ar-saadi 52:35, the scholar our own teacher note cites) explains مِنْ غَيْرِ شَيْءٍ as «لا خالق خلقهم، بل وجدوا من غير إيجاد ولا موجد». Ibn Kathir (en-ibn-kathir 52:35-36): "were they created without a maker or did they create themselves... Allah is the One Who created them and brought them into existence after they were nothing." Abdel Haleem: "created without any agent"; Taqi Usmani: "created by none"; Hilali-Khan and Sahih International: "created by nothing". Al-Tabari gives two other readings (without parents; for no purpose), and none of the three is creation from no material.
- **why:** In English, "made from nothing" means without material. The tafsir we cite reads it as without a maker. Framed as an impossible option, it teaches the learner that creation from nothing cannot happen, yet Ahl al-Sunnah hold that Allah creates after non-existence (Ibn Kathir's own words above). An Azhari teacher would not sign "something can't come from nothing" with no qualifier: the impossible thing is coming into being with no one bringing it into being. A sharp teen will also use the slip against the lesson on p10 ("so how did Allah make the universe from nothing?").
- **fix:** p6 meaning: "Were they created by nothing, with no one making them? Or did they make themselves?" p5 item 1: "Nothing made it: it just appeared with no cause". p5 explain line 2: "Nothing made it: real nothing has no energy, no laws, no time. Nothing can't do anything, so nothing can't make a universe." p12 card 5: "Something can begin with nothing at all making it happen". Add one sentence to the p6 explain: "The ayah doesn't say Allah can't create from nothing. He does. It says nothing can appear with no Creator."
- **refuter:** p6 meaning, p5 item 1 and p12 card 5 all read "from nothing" as "without material", while al-Sa'di and Ibn Kathir (connector) read it as "without a maker"; framing it as impossible clashes with creation after non-existence.
