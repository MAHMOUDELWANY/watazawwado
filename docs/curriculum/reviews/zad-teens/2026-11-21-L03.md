### subject-scholar-1 · judgement · status: open
- **where:** L3 p12 (sort card 5) and p7 explain line 1, plus the matching Arabic note p7 (course)
- **evidence:** Card: "Obeying a desire over Allah gives it the place of a god" → bin `fact`. p7 explain: "A "god" (ilah) is whatever you obey, love and fear most." Arabic p7: «"الإله" هو اللي بتطيعه وبتحبه وبتخاف منه أكتر حاجة». The tafsir does not support this. Al-Sa'di on 45:23 describes «الرجل الضال» whose rule is whatever he desires «سواء كان يرضي الله أو يسخطه». Al-Muyassar: «فلا يهوى شيئًا إلا فَعَله», and he reads the ayah for believers as a warning: «والآية أصل في التحذير من أن يكون الهوى هو الباعث للمؤمنين على أعمالهم». The ayah itself goes on to «وَأَضَلَّهُ ٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ عِلْمٍ وَخَتَمَ عَلَىٰ سَمْعِهِۦ وَقَلْبِهِۦ».
- **why:** In Ahl al-Sunnah creed, giving in to a desire against Allah's command once, or often, is a sin (ma'siyah). It does not set up a god beside Allah unless the person makes his desire his law in everything, or worships it with the love, humility and devotion due to Allah. The p8 slide gets this right: "when one *always* wins". The card drops the "always" and makes it a plain *fact* that any act of obeying a desire over Allah gives it the place of a god. That is the Khawarij path: every sin becomes shirk. For a 13-year-old who has just admitted on p7 which pull is hardest for them, it reads as "I am a mushrik", which is wrong creed and a needless shame trigger. The p7 definition has the same gap: the ilah is the one *worshipped* (al-ma'luh, al-ma'bud), as al-Muyassar's «لا معبود بحق» shows. "Obey most" alone does not make something an ilah; Muslims obey parents and teachers too.
- **fix:** Card → "Letting a desire *always* win over Allah gives it a place that belongs only to Him" (fact), or swap in a clean card: "Every sin makes a person a mushrik" (myth). p7 explain → "An ilah is whatever a person worships: loves, fears, hopes in and obeys the way only Allah deserves. For most people today that isn't a statue. A desire becomes that when it wins every time, even against Allah." Add one teacher line on p8: "Giving in to a desire is a sin you repent from, not shirk. The ayah describes someone whose desire has become his rule in everything." Arabic notes the same: «الإله هو اللي بيتعبد: بيتحب ويتخاف منه ويترجى ويتطاع زي ما ربنا بس يستحق… والمعصية ذنب نتوب منه، مش شرك».
- **refuter:** card 5 (line 307) drops the slide's "always" and makes any desire obeyed over Allah a god, which equates sin with shirk; p7 note's "obey most" definition shares the gap.
