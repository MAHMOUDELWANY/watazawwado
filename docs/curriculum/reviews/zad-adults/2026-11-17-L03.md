### subject-scholar-1 · confirmed · status: open
- **where:** L3 p12 s3 (all viewports), with p12 tadabbur question and p6 s3, p13 explain 2, p21 card 8
- **evidence:** p12 line 3: "Brady and Carrey described the same thing, fourteen centuries later, in their own words." The
"same thing" is the ma'ishah dank of Ta-Ha 20:124. Ibn Kathir (quran.com/20:123/tafsirs/169) defines the subject of the
threat: "Whoever opposes my command and what I have revealed to My Messenger ... taken his guidance from other than it",
and the tightness is "due to his misguidance". Al-Sa'di (quran.com/20:124/tafsirs/91) reads the i'rad as leaving the
Book "على وجه الإعراض ... أو ... الإنكار له والكفر به" and says of the tight life "ولا يكون ذلك إلا عذابا".
- **why:** The slide hands two named, living men the description of a punishment ayah, i.e. it declares their inner state to be
Allah's penalty for rejecting revelation. A scholar does not pronounce that on a named living person, and it breaks the
Adults rule "no mockery of anyone" in spirit: the lesson quoted them "for their honesty" and then uses their honesty as
proof of their punishment. Second problem: the same ayah is then turned on the believing learner ("Where in my life do I
feel squeezed?") while p17 and p18 tell Sara and Adam that their emptiness is the fitrah and not a sign of fake iman. The
lesson says both "your hollowness is the fitrah asking" and "hollowness is the ma'ishah dank of those who turn from His
remembrance" without separating them. Ibn Kathir himself narrows the threat ("not included in the meaning of this
specific threat") for a Muslim who still acts on the Quran.
- **fix:** p12 line 3 → "Many who seem to have everything describe this tightness in their own words." (no names). Add to
p12 explain: "Ibn Kathir and al-Sa'di read the ayah first about the one who rejects the revelation. A believer who grows
heedless can taste a share of this tightness; that application is ours, not their wording." Tadabbur question → "Where
have I turned away from His remembrance, and did the tightness follow?" p21 card 8 why → "Filling the place with things:
the hunger in the two quotes." (drop any link to the ayah's penalty).
- **refuter:** p12 line 3 does put two named living men under the punishment ayah, and the same ayah is then turned on the believing learner with no line separating the two; Ibn Kathir's and al-Sa'di's framing is as quoted.
- **also raised by:** story-editor-6

### teacher-parent-1 · judgement · status: open

Do not diagnose. If there is any risk of self-harm, give the local emergency number."
