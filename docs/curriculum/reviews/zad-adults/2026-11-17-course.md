### course-1 · major · confirmed
where: course (L2 p1, p2, p11, p21; L3 p1, p2, p16, p18, p22)
evidence: See the callback ledger. L2 has 4 callbacks to L1 that the learner can see on a slide. L3 has 6 to L1 and L2. None of these 10 asks the learner to recall anything from memory: each either tells (L2 p11 l4 "Lesson 1 called this the gap"; L3 p16 "Last week, the world outside…") or reads from the open notebook (L2 p1, L3 p1; the drawn notebook already shows the line, `L3\steps\p01.png` tile 2). L3's only content callback to L1 on a slide is the p18 reveal "Hanzalah's rule too: an hour and an hour" (l03 line 454). In l03, grep finds 0 hits for "Ihsan", "level", "ladder", "Takathur", "Jibril" and "see Him". No review game (L1 p23, L2 p22, L3 p21) has a card from an earlier lesson.
why: The owner wants one web. Memory builds a web when earlier material is recalled in new settings: the testing effect (Roediger & Karpicke 2006), spacing (Cepeda et al. 2006), and building on what the learner already knows (Bransford, Brown & Cocking 2000). What the course has is a chain: each lesson nods to the one before, by telling. Two lessons on, Lesson 1's ideas are gone. The cause is that no rule asks for recall, or for links further back than one lesson.
fix: Apply the "Accumulation design" section below: about 20 line-level edits across L2–L3, no new pages. Add the web rule (course-14).

**The web rule (for the curriculum-builder, every lesson from Lesson 2)**
1. Spark: one closed-book question on the last lesson and one on a lesson two or more back, answered before the notebook opens. Then the habit check, naming last week's options.
2. Zad: at least two slide lines that name an earlier lesson ("Lesson N…") and add something to it, plus at least one ask the learner answers from memory. Telling in `explain` does not count.
3. A recurring model keeps its glossary name (from the MAP) and its shared drawing, with today's place on it lit.
4. Every hook planted for the next lesson (a named case person, an image, an ayah, a promise) goes in the brief's "Callbacks" table. The next lesson's brief says where each one is paid off. An unpaid hook fails review.
5. The review has at least two cards from earlier lessons, and one case per lesson needs a model from an earlier lesson.

## Verdict on accumulation
**Partly, and weakening.** The fixed rituals work. Each lesson opens on the last one's question and notebook line, and each hand-off is answered the next week. The drawn notebook adds one entry per lesson. L2→L3 is a real two-part argument (outside, then inside; mind, then heart), and L3 p16 and p23 make it visible.

But every link is one lesson back, and every link is told, never recalled: no item in L2 or L3 asks the learner to remember anything. By L3, Lesson 1's main ideas have vanished: the three levels, the steps of certainty, At-Takathur, and Ihsan's "He sees you". Yet L3's question is exactly what those ideas were built to answer. The recurring models change their picture each time they come back. The hooks L2 planted for L3 (Sami, the city glow, 52:36) are dropped. After three weeks, a learner will remember three good lessons, not one structure.
