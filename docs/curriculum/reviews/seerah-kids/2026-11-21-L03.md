### age-band-2 · judgement · status: open
- **where:** L3 p0 title, p6 s1, cover/tent/staff lamps
- **evidence:** Title "Three Goodbyes"; p6: "That was the second goodbye. Two lamps had gone out."; three lamps on the cover, one still burning at the end of the deaths, never explained (see curriculum-designer-4).
- **why:** Young children take euphemisms literally ("goodbye" suggests someone left and may come back; a lamp "going out" is a metaphor a 6-year-old may not map to death). The bereavement guidance above recommends plain words: "died", "dead", "will not come back". The lesson does say "died" on p3 and p6, which is right, but wraps it in two metaphors. Worse, a child counting lamps is left waiting for the third to go out, and the next lamp holder is Abu Talib, whom the boy clings to on p13 ("Take me with you!"): the device primes the fear that the next carer dies too.
- **fix:** Keep "died" every time and make the metaphor explicit once on p3: "When someone dies, we say goodbye. They do not come back in this life. The first lamp went out." Resolve the count inside the lesson (curriculum-designer-4) and end the lamp device with a new lamp lighting on p7: "And a new light came into his life: Uncle Abu Talib." so the last lamp image the child sees is one being lit, not one waiting to go out.
- **raised as:** major
- **refuter:** The lesson already says "died" plainly on p3 and p6 as the finding concedes, and the unresolved third lamp is carried by curriculum-designer-4; only the euphemism layer and the fear-of-the-next-carer point are new.
