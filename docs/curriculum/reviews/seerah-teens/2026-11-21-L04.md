### age-band-1 · judgement · status: open
- **where:** L4 goal, p11, p12 (both learners)
- **evidence:** Goal: "why honest people recognise the truth when it walks past them". p11: "Knowledge alone is not enough. Many people know the truth and keep quiet." p12 ayah meaning "yet a group of them hide the truth while they know it", lines "Bahira was one who recognised and spoke. The ayah warns about those who recognise and hide. The difference between them was never knowledge. It was honesty." p12 has no teacher note; the only interfaith guard is on p13 ("Keep the warning historical…").
- **why:** The lesson draws a line from "people of the Book who did not follow" to "dishonest", and stops nowhere. The 13-year-old will apply it to the Christian friend sitting next to him on Monday: "she knows it's true and she's hiding it". That turns a tafsir point about the scholars of that time who knew the description (Al-Tafsir al-Muyassar, as the file itself quotes) into a verdict on ordinary people today, and it harms the friendship the teen most needs to handle well. The convert hears that his Christian parents are either ignorant or liars, which is shame, and exactly what keeps converts from bringing their families near. The goal sentence states the converse for everyone: whoever does not recognise is not honest.
- **fix:** (1) Goal: "…what the monk Bahira saw, and what it takes to say the truth you know." (2) p12 line 3: "The ayah is about scholars of that time who knew the description and hid it. It is not a verdict on every Christian or Jew you know." (3) Teacher note on p12, EN and AR: "If the learner asks about Christian friends or family: the Quran praises sincere Christians (5:82–83, subject-scholar to verify through the connector) and Bahira himself was one. - **refuter:** p11, p12 and the goal draw the line from "recognise and hide" to dishonesty with no note on p12 (only p13 carries a guard), and the cited 5:82–83 does praise Christians "devoted to learning and ascetics" (Abdel Haleem, connector), though 5:82 also names the Jews as most hostile, so the teacher note must quote it with care.

### learning-scientist-1 · confirmed · status: open
- **where:** L4 p0–p2, p15 (course)
- **evidence:** Ledger above. L4 p0 explain "Recap in one line: an orphan, a grandfather gone, an uncle who took him in." L4 p2 "His nephew was about twelve. He had lost his mother, then his grandfather." L1 p1 "Byzantine Rome in the west" is never connected to p4 "a Roman frontier town". All six p15 cards are L4 facts or morals.
- **why:** Retrieval practice spaced across sessions is the most robust memory effect in the literature (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006, *Psychological Science*; Cepeda et al., 2006, *Psychological Bulletin*). Re-telling the recap to the learner is re-study, which feels fluent and produces much weaker retention. The end of a block is the natural point for cumulative retrieval; this lesson does none, and the owner's own example ("a later lesson that never says 'as we mentioned in Lesson 1'") is exactly this.
- **fix:** Insert a "Story so far" page as p1 (`station: 'spark'`, `layout: 'question'`, `interaction: ask`), three asks answered before each reveal: (1) "Busra was ruled by one of the two empires from Episode 1. Which one?" reveal: "Byzantine Rome, the empire in the west." (2) "Who took the boy in after his grandfather died, and how long did he protect him?" reveal: "Abu Talib, for forty years (Episode 3)." (3) "Before Bahira, one other person saw something special in this child when nobody else wanted him. Who?" reveal: "Halimah (Episode 2): the blessing that followed him." Then cut p2 line 1 (the learner just produced it). In the check, add one step "Put the four episodes in order" (the elephant · Halimah's donkey · Abdul-Muttalib's mattress · the monk's meal).
- **refuter:** p0 and p2 re-tell L3 and the p15 cards are all L4, so there is no retrieval of L1–L3 at the end of the first block.
- **also raised by:** story-editor-4
