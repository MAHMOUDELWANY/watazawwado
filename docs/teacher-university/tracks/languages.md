# Track: Languages (Fusha, Egyptian Arabic, English)

DRAFT. This is the track the modules were written closest to. Most ideas apply directly.

| Module | What it means here |
|---|---|
| 1 Know your learner | CEFR level from a real talk, goal situations (work, family, travel, religion), home language. |
| 2 Plan the lesson | Objective with an action and a context: "Order food politely in Fusha using three phrases and one follow-up question." |
| 3 Frameworks | All five apply. Choose by aim: PPP for one item, TTT to find gaps, TBLT for speaking, text-based for reading and listening. |
| 4 Meaning | MFP plus appropriacy for phrases; Egyptian Arabic is heavy on function and formality, so always use a clear situation. |
| 5 Rules | Guided discovery with examples first. Do not pile on grammar terms. |
| 6 Reading and listening | Real audio with graded tasks. Teach how to listen for stressed words. |
| 7 Speaking and writing | The full structure; choose a genre the learner will really need. |
| 8 Correction | Full decision flow. Kids: on the spot and playful. |
| 9 Sound | Teach how Arabic sounds are made for English-background learners; for Egyptian, add the rhythm of the dialect. |
| 10 Revision | Vocabulary journal, spaced revisit, short games. |
| 11 Beliefs, L1 | For Fusha and Egyptian Arabic, English lowers stress for beginners; move toward Arabic as the learner grows. For English lessons, Arabic helps with false friends and missing sounds. |
| 12 Growth | Learn another language yourself, to feel what your learners feel. |

## Notes by language
- **Fusha:** diacritics and case endings need a deliberate plan; decide with the curriculum how far to take them at each level.
- **Egyptian Arabic:** register matters (what is said with family, at work, with strangers); teach the situation with the phrase.
- **English:** many of our learners are Arabic speakers; use L1 to predict errors (sounds, articles, word order).
