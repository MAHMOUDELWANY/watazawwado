# Module 10: Revision, recycling and homework

**Why it matters.** Most of what a learner meets in a lesson fades within a day unless it is revisited. Teaching without revision is filling a bucket with a hole.

## The ideas

### 1. Revise regularly, in spaced steps
Research on forgetting is consistent: a lot is lost within 24 hours, and it keeps going unless the material comes back. So revisit not only last lesson's content but also last week's, last month's, even last year's. Keep a running list of what to bring back, and use the shared notes.

### 2. Make it meaningful
Learners who connect words to their own life or to a story remember more than learners who memorise a list, even when the second group knows a test is coming. The brain needs to **work on** the item: sort it, imagine using it, link it to a person or a place.
Simple ways: ask them to put the words into their own categories; to use them in a sentence about their life; to tell a story with them; to link them with a movement or a sound.

### 3. Make it engaging
Short games lift the mood and still revise. Example: *two truths and a lie* with the target words, each side writes three sentences and the other guesses the lie. It also builds rapport.
After teaching a topic, do not just recall words. Ask: *"Tell me about your own experience of this."*

### 4. Revise in chunks
A word is more useful with the words it normally goes with. Ask: *"What does this usually appear with?"*

### 5. Do not forget to plan it
In short or goal-focused courses (an exam, a deadline), revision is the first thing dropped. Plan a few minutes in every lesson.

### 6. Homework: ask first
Talk about it early: Do they want homework? How often? How much time is realistic? What tasks do they enjoy, and which do they dislike?

### 7. A good homework task is
- **Well set up:** exactly what to do, in what format, with a tip.
- **Relevant:** connected to their real needs. A business learner practising the past tense writes about previous jobs, not a holiday.
- **Level-appropriate:** even with real material, the *task* is graded.
- **Realistic:** if they say "an hour a week", ask when exactly.
- **Enjoyable:** a challenge, a topic they love, a chance to teach you something.
- **Explained:** when they know why they are doing it and what feedback they will get, they are more motivated.

### 8. Many skills in one task
A task that combines reading, listening, speaking and writing gives more brain work. Match the task to the person: a child may draw and describe, an adult may research and report, a teenager may record a short video.

### 9. Systems that help
- A short **homework conversation** at the start of a course.
- A **shared tracker** (date, task, done, feedback, next step).
- A **check-in** at the start of each lesson: how was it, what was hard, fix a section together.
- A **reflection** for the learner: what did you enjoy, what was hard, what would you like next.
(A ready template is in [templates.md](../templates.md).)

### 10. Using AI tools for ideas (carefully)
An AI assistant can draft homework ideas quickly. Give it the lesson topic, the aim, the learner's level and interests, and what you want them to practise. Always check the result and adjust it: it is a draft, not a plan. Do not paste private learner information into outside tools. Follow the academy's data rules.

## In our lessons
- **Hifz:** this module is the same logic as the revision structure of hifz: new portion, recent revision, older revision. Plan all three every lesson. The spaced rule is why a "revision-only" lesson is not wasted time.
- **Quran / Tajweed:** recycle a rule in a new passage, not only the one where it was taught.
- **Zad Path:** revisit last week's story by retelling it from a different person's view.
- **Languages:** keep a vocabulary journal; revisit it at spaced intervals.

## Try it
Choose a topic from three weeks ago. Plan a 5-minute revisit that makes the learner *do* something with it (sort, retell, use in a sentence). Use it and ask them how much they remembered.

## Check yourself
1. Why revisit content from last month?
   *Without spacing, memory fades.*
2. Which helps memory more: memorising a list, or deciding how you would use each word?
   *Deciding how to use each word (deeper processing).*
3. Give two qualities of a good homework task.
   *Relevant, well explained, level-appropriate, realistic, enjoyable (any two).*
4. Is AI-written homework ready to give as it is?
   *No: always check and adapt.*

## Reflect
What is the first thing you cut when time runs short? How would your learners' progress change if it were protected?
