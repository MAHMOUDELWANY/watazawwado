# Module 6: Reading and listening lessons (receptive skills)

**Why it matters.** Knowing the words and rules does not make a person a good reader or listener. Understanding a text is its own skill, and it can be taught.

## The ideas

### 1. Three ways to read
We read differently depending on the goal:
- **Skimming:** fast, for the general idea (a film review before buying a ticket).
- **Scanning:** fast, for one fact (a train time).
- **Detail reading:** slow and careful (a story where every clue matters).
Match the task to the text. Do not give every text the same list of questions.

### 2. Why listening is hard
Common problems: speed, words that run together, accents, background noise, poor audio, no context. The fix is practice with many voices and situations, plus teaching how to listen: focus on key words, let the rest go. Do not only test; train.

### 3. Real material, graded task
Material made for native speakers is messy and uses language above the learner's level. It prepares them for real life, and avoids the shock of "I understood class but not the film".
Golden rule: **grade the task, not the text.** The same audio can ask a beginner to list what they hear and an advanced learner to explain why a speaker disagreed.
Some learners panic when they miss words. Real material trains tolerance for not understanding everything.

### 4. A lesson shape that works
1. **Lead-in.** Interest and background knowledge.
2. **Orientation.** Say what the text is, who is speaking, where.
3. **Gist.** One quick question on the main idea (with a time limit for reading).
4. **Pre-teach (optional).** Only the words that would block the detail task. Not too many; guessing from context is a skill to build.
5. **Detail.** Focused questions. If they cannot answer, replay or re-read that part and help; do not let them struggle too long.
6. **Follow-up.** Speaking or writing about the text, or a focus on a language point from it.

A good gist question is about the whole message ("Was the speaker satisfied? Why?"), not a small fact.

## In our lessons
- **Quran:** the "text" is the ayah and its recitation. Listening is listening to a model reciter, then to the learner's own recording. Gist: what is this passage about? Detail: where did the reciter stop, which sound did they hold?
- **Zad Path:** a short Seerah passage or hadith as the reading text. Always show the text first. Gist question first, a few words pre-taught, a detail question, then a discussion or a "what would you have done" task.
- **Languages:** short real audio and articles with graded tasks; teach the learner to listen for stressed words.

## Try it
Pick a 1 to 2 minute audio or short passage. Write one gist question, three detail questions, and the two words you will pre-teach. Check: would a learner at a lower level also be able to do a version of the task?

## Check yourself
1. Which skill is "reading a timetable to find a time"?
   *Scanning.*
2. What does "grade the task, not the text" mean?
   *Keep the real material but make the task fit the learner's level.*
3. Why limit pre-teaching?
   *Too many words steals the work of guessing and the focus of the lesson.*
4. What is a good gist question like?
   *About the main message of the whole text.*

## Reflect
When did you last give a text and only ask comprehension questions? What could you have taught about *how* to read or listen?
