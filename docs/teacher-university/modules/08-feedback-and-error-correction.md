# Module 8: Feedback and error correction

**Why it matters.** Correcting everything crushes confidence. Correcting nothing lets mistakes harden. The skill is choosing.

## The ideas

### 1. Real error or slip?
- A **real error:** they do not know the correct form yet.
- A **slip:** they know it, but said it wrongly because they were fast, tired or focused on the meaning.
Slips need only a light reminder or nothing. Real errors are worth your time.
Typical causes of errors: the first language's influence; speed; over-applying a rule; language above their level.

### 2. What to correct
Correct:
- Errors connected to the **aim of the lesson**.
- Errors that **block understanding** even if unrelated to the aim.
You can let go of:
- Slips.
- Errors far above the learner's level.
Correcting every mistake discourages learners, especially beginners.

### 3. The decision, in order
1. Real error or slip?
2. Does it make the meaning hard to understand?
3. Is the activity about accuracy or fluency?
4. Is it linked to the point of the lesson?
5. Is it appropriate for their level?
Result: correct now, note it for later, or let it go.

### 4. On-the-spot techniques
- Give the correct form.
- Stress the error with your voice.
- Offer two versions and ask which is right.
- Mime or draw.
- Finger count to show where the problem is.
- A short metalanguage hint.
Always **try to get self-correction first**: a look, a repeat of their words, a question. The goal is that they can fix it when you are not there.

### 5. Four moves for language that appears in speech
1. **Retrieve:** write down or note what they just said.
2. **Repeat:** say it again, have them repeat it.
3. **Recast:** say it back in a better form, without calling it a mistake.
4. **Recycle:** have them use it again in a new sentence or situation.

### 6. Delayed correction
First respond to the **content**: "That sounds like a good weekend." Then, after they finish, work on the language. Ways to do it on a shared screen:
- Two columns: "Good language" and "To improve." They sort the sentences.
- "What you said" vs "A more natural way."
- Group errors by type: grammar, vocabulary, sound.
- Error dictation: read a sentence, they write a tick or the corrected version.
- Error correction as homework: they review their own notes.

### 7. Children respond differently
Children usually do not reflect on their own language, so delayed correction rarely works. Correct on the spot, lightly and playfully.

### 8. Be honest about level
If an error is above their current level, say so: "This is a hard one, we will come back to it later." It is fine, and it builds trust.

## In our lessons
- **Quran / Tajweed:** mistakes in recitation are often best corrected on the spot because the sound is the point; but protect their flow in a longer reading by noting, then returning. Match the **correction to the aim**: if the lesson is about one rule, mark that rule first.
- **Hifz:** a mistake in memorised text must be corrected in the same moment and re-recited, because a wrong repetition becomes habit.
- **Zad Path:** correct wrong understanding gently and with a question, never with ridicule. For children, praise effort first.
- **Languages:** use the full decision above.

## Try it
For one lesson, keep a two-column note: *good language* and *to improve*. At the end, show it to the learner and ask them to fix the second column. Note how many they fix without your help.

## Check yourself
1. What is a slip?
   *A mistake the learner knows how to avoid, made through speed or focus on meaning.*
2. Which errors should you always consider correcting?
   *Those linked to the lesson aim and those that block understanding.*
3. Why try self-correction first?
   *It builds the ability to fix it alone.*
4. Which correction style suits children best?
   *On the spot, light and playful.*

## Reflect
When did you last correct something that you now think you could have left? What would have happened if you had waited?
