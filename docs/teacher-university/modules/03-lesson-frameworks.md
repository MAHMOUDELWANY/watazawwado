# Module 3: Lesson frameworks

**Why it matters.** A framework is a reliable order for a lesson. You do not need to invent the shape each time, and you can choose the shape that suits the learner and the aim.

## The ideas

### 1. PPP: Present, Practise, Produce
1. **Lead-in.** Warm the topic and wake up what they know.
2. **Present** the target (new language or rule) in context: meaning, form, sound.
3. **Controlled practice.** Tasks with mostly one right answer: gap-fill, matching, repeat-and-fix.
4. **Freer practice.** The learner uses it in their own words: role-play, retell, write.

Strengths: clear, quick, good for a single new item. Risk: the lesson becomes teacher-led, and the free stage gets squeezed.
Timing tip: aim for about 20% present, 30% controlled, 50% free, with feedback running through the last part.
Always plan from the **free task** backwards.

### 2. TTT: Test, Teach, Test
1. Lead-in.
2. **Test 1.** A task that needs the target, to see what they already can do.
3. **Teach** only what the first task showed they need.
4. **Test 2.** Controlled practice, then a freer use.

Strengths: respects what they know, saves time. Risk: you must think fast about what to teach. Success depends on choosing a first task that really calls for the target.

### 3. TBLT: Task-Based
Start from a real task with a clear outcome ("agree on a plan", "solve this", "present this"). The teacher watches, then works on the language that task revealed. Steps: topic, set up the task, learner prepares, **task**, analyse together, practise the useful language, **repeat the task** with a twist.
Think of it as PPP turned upside down. A good task is relevant, engaging, life-like, has a goal, and is about meaning more than about a grammar form.

### 4. Text-based presentation
The target language comes from a text, audio or video. Stages: lead-in, orientation (tell them what the text is), read or listen for the main idea, **clarify the target language from the text**, controlled practice, freer practice.
Watch the clock: do not spend the whole lesson on the text and leave no time for the learner to speak.

### 5. Flipped lesson
The learner meets some content before the lesson (a short text, a few words, a rule), and live time is for practice and speaking. Good for building habits and confidence. It depends on the learner actually doing the prework, so agree it clearly.

### 6. Choosing
Ask:
- One new item, or a skill? (item: PPP or TTT; skill: text-based or task-based)
- Do I know what they need, or do I need to find out? (find out: TTT or TBLT)
- How much time do they have outside the lesson? (a lot: flipped)
- How confident are they? (low: more present and control first)

You can mix. A PPP mini-cycle inside a task-based lesson is normal.

## In our lessons

| Framework | Quran / Tajweed | Zad Path | Languages |
|---|---|---|---|
| PPP | Present a rule with a model recitation, drill it in isolation, then read a passage that contains it | Present a short story point or etiquette, check understanding, then role-play or retell | New phrase set, drill, role-play |
| TTT | Listen to the learner recite first, find the real problem, teach it, listen again | Ask what they already know about the event or practice, teach the gaps, ask again | Speak first, teach the gaps, speak again |
| Text-based | A short ayah or dua as the text: orient, listen, then focus on a rule inside it | A Seerah passage or hadith (text first, never a paraphrase before the text) | Short article or audio |
| TBLT | Rare; use for explaining a ruling to a family member | "Explain it to a younger sibling" tasks | Plan a trip, solve a problem |
| Flipped | Listen to the model recording before the lesson | Read the short passage at home | Prework vocabulary or listening |

Note for Quran and Zad Path: when a lesson centres on a text from the Quran or hadith, **show the text first**, then work on it. This is a standing rule of our curriculum.

## Try it
Choose a topic you are about to teach. Plan it twice, once as PPP and once as TTT, in two lines each. Which would suit this learner better today, and why?

## Check yourself
1. In TTT, what decides the content of the Teach stage?
   *What the first task showed the learner could not yet do.*
2. What is the main risk of text-based lessons?
   *Running out of time for the learner to use the language.*
3. How is TBLT different from PPP?
   *It starts with the task and works on language afterwards.*
4. Why plan backwards from the free task?
   *So every earlier stage supports it, and it is not squeezed out.*

## Reflect
Which framework do you use almost without thinking? What would happen if you tried a different one once this week?
