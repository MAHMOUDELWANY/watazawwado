# Module 9: Teaching sound (pronunciation)

**Why it matters.** Pronunciation is more than single sounds. Rhythm, stress and melody shape whether a person is understood. And for the Quran, sound is the heart of the subject.

## The ideas

### 1. Sounds and the first language
Each language has its own set of sounds. A sound that exists in the learner's first language is easy. A sound that does not exist is hard, and the learner swaps it for the nearest one they know.
If you know their first language, point out what is shared and what is new. If you do not, listen carefully and imitate their sound yourself, then compare.

### 2. Teach how a sound is made
"Listen and repeat" alone does not help with a sound the mouth has never produced. Explain or show:
- Is the mouth open or closed? Wide or round?
- Where is the tongue?
- Does air flow freely or is there a block?
- Do the vocal cords vibrate?
Then the learner can rely on muscle memory, not only on hearing.

### 3. Drilling: useful, but not alone
Repetition is good for memory and automatic speech, especially when combined with the physical explanation. Vary it so it stays light: chain drills, short chunks, playful challenges.

### 4. Words and sentences
- **Word stress:** which syllable is stronger. It changes between languages, and learners usually carry the pattern of their own.
- **Sentence stress:** key words are stronger than small ones.
- **Contrastive stress:** changing the stressed word changes the meaning.
- **Intonation and rhythm:** the "music" of speech. Some languages are syllable-timed and flatter, some stress-timed and more varied.
- Tonal languages: pitch changes meaning, so make it visible (rising, falling, flat).
Use colours on the shared screen to mark stressed syllables.

### 5. Activities that work
- **Shadowing:** read a short text aloud, the learner follows silently in their head, then together.
- **Drama and role:** speaking as someone else reduces self-consciousness.
- **Chunking:** build a phrase backwards in small steps.
- **Contrastive stress:** play with one sentence and different emphasis.
- **Poetry:** rhythm and vocabulary together.

### 6. Priorities
Not every error needs fixing. Start with those that could make them misunderstood. Decide with the learner how far to go. Accent is part of identity; the target is to be **clearly understood**, not to hide who you are. Keep it light and praise success. Learners are often self-conscious.

### 7. Find the real cause
A mispronounced word might come from a missing sound, the wrong stress, spelling confusion, or the way a letter changes with the next letter. Diagnose before you drill.

## In our lessons
- **Quran / Tajweed:** this module is the bridge to **makharij and sifat**. The same method applies: where the sound is made, how the air and voice behave, then model, imitate, drill, apply in a word, then in a line. Keep correction specific ("tongue position", "how long you hold"), and keep it kind. Where a ruling or a term has a defined meaning in tajweed, always use the curriculum's definition.
- **Zad Path:** reciting dua and adhkar correctly; same steps, lighter.
- **Languages:** the full module. For Arabic learners with an English background, expect difficulty with sounds that English lacks; teach the physical production, not only the sound.

## Try it
Choose one sound your learner finds hard. Write a three-line explanation of how it is made (mouth, tongue, air or voice). Teach it with the explanation instead of only repetition, and compare the result.

## Check yourself
1. Why is "listen and repeat" often not enough?
   *It does not teach the physical production of a new sound.*
2. Name two parts of pronunciation beyond single sounds.
   *Word stress and sentence stress, intonation and rhythm.*
3. How do you decide which errors to correct first?
   *Those that cause misunderstanding.*
4. What are the first things to diagnose when a word is mispronounced?
   *Missing sound, wrong stress, spelling effects, and letter changes.*

## Reflect
Which sound do your learners struggle with most? What have you tried, and did you teach how it is *made*?
