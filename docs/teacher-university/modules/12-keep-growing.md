# Module 12: Keep growing (self-directed professional development)

**Why it matters.** The best teachers do not stop learning. Growth you choose yourself is the kind that lasts, protects against burnout, and shows in your lessons.

## The ideas

### 1. What professional development is
It is ongoing learning that helps you understand and improve your practice. It is **not only about fixing problems**. You can build on strengths, satisfy curiosity or try something new. "Practice" is wider than teaching: how you work with learners, parents and colleagues, and how you look after your own time and wellbeing.

### 2. Self-directed means you choose
You decide what to learn, why, when, where, with whom, and how. Even where some development is required, there is often room to shape it. Ask the six questions: **what, why, when, where, with whom, how**.
Your options are wider than courses: peer observation, a small project with a colleague, a reading group, a teaching diary, a recorded lesson, a webinar, a book, learning a new skill as a learner yourself.

### 3. Three things decide what is possible
- **You:** your attitude, motivation, career stage.
- **Your context:** time, money, family, how supportive the workplace is.
- **The opportunities:** what exists and how good it is.
So there is no single right path. A good activity suits your situation now.

### 4. Is this activity good for me? Eight questions
Can I bring my own experience? Does it leave room for my own thinking? Does it meet a real need or curiosity? Can I take part and reflect, not only listen? Is the content rich and relevant? Do I get time to think how to use it? Does it open more to explore?

### 5. The seven-step cycle
1. **Reflect** on your current situation.
2. **Set goals** and purpose.
3. **Choose** an activity.
4. **Do** the activity.
5. **Reflect or discuss** it.
6. **Try it** in a real lesson.
7. **Reflect or discuss** what happened.
The steps are a cycle: skip some, repeat some, go back. Step 7 often starts a new cycle.

### 6. Step 1 tool: SWOT
Four boxes: **Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats**, about you and your context. Start with strengths and opportunities, since they motivate. Weaknesses and threats show what to plan around.

### 7. Step 2 tools: four parts of teaching, and topic areas
You can aim at: **awareness** (noticing), **attitudes** (how you think and feel), **knowledge** (what you know) or **skills** (what you do). Awareness and attitudes are the deepest and hardest to see.
Or choose a topic: yourself as a teacher, your learners, aspects of teaching, tools for teaching, your context.

### 8. Step 3 tool: score your options
For each possible activity, score 1 to 5 on four things: **purpose, resources, autonomy, enjoyment**. The highest total is the best fit for now. Redo it when life changes.

### 9. Step 5 to 7: a reflection page
Write: what I noticed; what I want to try; the idea in practice: when and where, what happened, why I think so, **how I know** (evidence), what is next.

### 10. Pace yourself
Pressure to always improve can cause stress. Sometimes "more of the same" is right for now. Notice your limits. Growth is a long game, and it works better with company: talk to a trusted colleague.

## In the academy
Ideas that fit us:
- **Peer observation:** invite a colleague into one lesson with a clear question ("watch only my questions").
- **Recorded lesson with consent:** ask the learner or parent first, say you will not share it, and ask them to say yes at the start. Watch it with one focus, using a self-observation sheet.
- **Lesson study pairs:** plan one lesson together, one teaches, both discuss.
- **A short monthly question** shared in the teachers' space.
This also links to the University's tracks: when you move to a new subject, the cycle above is the way to do it.

## Try it
Do a 10-minute SWOT for the next three months, then score three possible activities on purpose, resources, autonomy and enjoyment. Choose the highest and do it this month.
