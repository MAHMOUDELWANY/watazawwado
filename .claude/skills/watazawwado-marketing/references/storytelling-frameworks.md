# Storytelling and marketing frameworks (adapted to Watazawwado)

Each one: what it says, how we use it, where it stops working.

## STORIFY (Chris Hewitt) — our script spine
Someone (the hero: a mother, a young adult) · Tension (the feeling) · Obstacle (the stranger) · Risk (what happens if
nothing changes) · Intervention (the teacher who knows you) · FutureYou (the life after).
*Use:* every script and campaign. The brand appears at letter five, never first.
*Limit:* 30-second Shorts compress it to Hook (S+T) → Obstacle → Intervention → Close.

## Building a StoryBrand (Donald Miller)
Customer is the hero, the brand is the guide with empathy and authority, a plan, a call to action, stakes.
*Use:* website copy, the homepage sub-line, carousels. Empathy = "most parents I taught couldn't tell me who taught
their child"; authority = the ijazah.
*Limit:* can sound formulaic; keep our own words.

## Made to Stick (Heath & Heath) — SUCCESs
Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, Stories. Chapter 3: if a five-year-old can't picture it, you lose
the audience.
*Use:* the check on every line (decision framework Q4). "A mentor who stays" needs a scene: "same teacher, same
Tuesday, six months later he still remembers you love dinosaurs."
*Limit:* a checklist, not a strategy.

## How Brands Grow (Byron Sharp, Ehrenberg-Bass)
Grow by being easy to remember (mental availability) and easy to find; use distinctive assets consistently; reach
broadly.
*Use:* repeat the same assets everywhere, unchanged: the zipper sound, "Pack your zaad" + gesture, the zaad bag,
crimson, Zad. Consistency beats novelty.
*Limit:* evidence from big brands with paid reach; we use it for consistency, not for mass media.

## Contagious (Jonah Berger) — STEPPS
Social currency, Triggers, Emotion, Public, Practical value, Stories.
*Use:* "people start talking about us": share cards ("I packed my zaad"), seasonal triggers (Ramadan, holidays),
practical carousels parents save.
*Limit:* virality is not a plan; it rides on the steady pillars.

## Breakthrough Advertising (Eugene Schwartz) — awareness levels
Unaware → problem-aware → solution-aware → product-aware → most aware.
*Use:* pillar 2 and 3 reach the unaware; pillar 4 (parent playbook) the problem-aware; pillar 1 and the website the
solution- and product-aware.
*Limit:* no data behind it; a lens.

## Jobs to Be Done (Christensen, Moesta)
People "hire" a product for a job; forces: push, pull, anxiety, habit.
*Use:* customer psychology (STRATEGY.md §1) and the VOC questions. Parents hire us to "give my child a real
relationship with the deen without chasing anyone".
*Limit:* needs real interviews; ours are still thin.

## Influence (Robert Cialdini)
Reciprocity, liking, authority, social proof, unity, commitment, scarcity.
*Use:* give value first (reciprocity); the founder's warmth (liking); the ijazah (authority); "our families" (unity);
honest scarcity only (wave of 20, true).
*Limit:* never fake scarcity or social proof (content RULES §3).

## The Long and the Short of It (Binet & Field)
Brand building (emotional, broad, slow) vs activation (rational, narrow, fast).
*Use:* in the 90-day awareness phase most content builds the brand; activation is the waitlist and the wave
invitations.
*Limit:* ratios assume paid media.

## Obviously Awesome (April Dunford)
Position against the customer's real alternatives, not the competitor you imagine.
*Use:* STRATEGY.md §2 (YouTube/apps, doing nothing, the masjid, another academy).

## Peak-end rule (Kahneman)
People remember the peak and the end.
*Use:* BRAND.md §8 nine moments; the trial's first 10 minutes and the same-day report.

## Chris Hewitt's other rules we adopted
- Sell the transformation, but outcomes only in customers' words (content RULES §3.4).
- Say what you stand against and what you refuse to do.
- Information vs story: open a Tajweed fact with a scene.
- A little humour and a human behind the logo; never jokes about religion.
- Cover the logo: if a competitor could say it, rewrite it.
