# Mahmoud's on-camera voice

Ustadh Mahmoud teaches the way our curriculum teaches (owner, 2026-10-21): he keeps the learner **involved and
curious**, moves and gestures, asks questions, says something wise, and never says anything that is not true. His
words change with the moment; he is not a fixed set of catchphrases. This skill turns any content idea into lines
that sound like him talking to one person.

It sits inside `watazawwado-content` (read that first). It never overrides `docs/marketing/RULES.md`: the academy
speaks ("we", "our teachers"), he introduces himself as one of the academy's teachers, no fatwas, no unverified
Quran or hadith, no invented claims, no hype.

## 1. The six moves of every script

Use them in this order unless the format forbids it. A Short may use four of them; a long video uses all six.

1. **Pull them in.** Open with a question, a small surprise or a moment the viewer recognises, said straight into
   the lens. Never "Assalamu alaikum, today we will talk about…". Curiosity first, greeting at the end or in the
   caption.
2. **Talk to one person.** "You", never "you guys" or "viewers". For a parent: "your child", by situation, never by a
   stereotype.
3. **Make them do something.** Every 10–15 seconds the viewer acts: "Say it with me", "Put your hand on your throat",
   "Pause and try once", "Which one did you hear, one or two?". Teaching is a conversation, even on video.
4. **Show it with the body.** Each beat carries a stage cue for the hands, face or mouth (see §3). The gesture
   explains before the word does.
5. **One wise line.** Once per video, a short true sentence that a parent might repeat at dinner. Plain words, no
   proverb-dump, no exaggeration. It must follow from what was just taught, not be pasted on.
6. **Close warm and honest.** Repeat the one fix, then one gentle next step (the CTA from the calendar item). Never
   pressure, never fake urgency.

## 2. Choosing the words (be the master of expression)

Pick the register from the audience and the moment, then vary the wording: no opener, transition or closer may
repeat inside the same series or the same week.

| Audience | English register | Arabic register |
|---|---|---|
| Kids 6–11 (or parents playing it to them) | Very short sentences, everyday words, playful sounds, wonder ("Ready? Three, two…") | Egyptian, very warm, diminutives allowed («يا حبيبي», «يلا بينا») |
| Parents (main audience) | Calm, respectful, practical; speaks as a teacher who has seen many children, without claiming numbers | 50/50: simple Fusha with a warm Egyptian touch (root RULES.md §6) |
| Adults and reverts | Reassuring, dignified, never childish; normalise starting from zero | Clear Fusha-leaning, gentle Egyptian colour |
| Arab diaspora parents | — | Egyptian-first, the way you talk to a cousin abroad; names the real feeling («ابنك بيرد عليك بالإنجليزي») |

Word rules:
- **Concrete over abstract.** "Your tongue touches the back of your top teeth" beats "correct articulation".
- **Arabic terms in English videos:** say the Arabic word, then the plain meaning once ("makhraj, the place a letter
  comes from"). Islamic phrases (InshaAllah, Alhamdulillah, ﷺ after the Prophet's name) used naturally, not as
  decoration.
- **Mood vocabulary:** he can be playful, tender, serious or amazed; choose the mood per beat and write it in the
  stage cue so the delivery matches the words.
- **Rhythm:** mix one-breath lines with an occasional longer sentence; read every line aloud in your head, and if it
  cannot be said in one breath, cut it.
- **Banned:** hype ("amazing", "life-changing", "game-changer"), AI tells ("let's dive in", "unlock", "journey",
  "it's not X, it's Y" reveals, triads for rhythm), preachy guilt ("if you don't… you will regret"), fear about the
  child, comparisons with other academies, any credential or level claim, any number we cannot prove.

## 3. Stage cues (body language)

Write them in square brackets in the Shot/Delivery column, short and filmable:

- **Hands:** [counts on fingers: 1… 2…], [points to the throat], [open palm toward camera: your turn], [small "stop"
  hand: wait, listen again], [taps the Mushaf line with the pen].
- **Face:** [raised eyebrows, curious], [big smile, proud of you], [leans in, quieter: here's the secret], [nods
  slowly], [mock-puzzled frown] (never mocking a person).
- **Mouth (recitation pillar):** [close-up on the lips], [slow motion of the letter twice].
- **Space:** [steps closer to the lens], [sits back, relaxed] for the wise line.
- **Pauses:** [beat] = one second of silence the editor keeps; use before the payoff and after a question.

Rule from `docs/marketing/RULES.md` §1.3: a "wrong vs right" demonstration uses practice syllables or isolated
letters, never an ayah read wrongly.

## 4. Script format (what goes into the Notion item page)

One table row per beat:

| Time | On-screen text | Spoken line (video language) | Delivery / shot | Viewer action |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Then: caption per platform, pinned comment, CTA with UTM link (`watazawwado-content` §3), and a line for the owner in
Arabic: «اللي محتاج تراجعه: …» (any Quran word, any claim, any line you might not say this way).
Give **two alternatives** for the hook and for the wise line, so he can pick the one that fits his mouth on the day.

## 5. Check before handing over

- [ ] Opens with curiosity, not a greeting; talks to one person.
- [ ] The viewer acts at least once per 15 seconds.
- [ ] Every beat has a delivery cue; the gestures are filmable by one person with a phone.
- [ ] Exactly one wise line, true and earned.
- [ ] Register matches the audience; no repeated opener or closer this week (check the week's other scripts).
- [ ] Nothing banned in §2; humanizer run; `docs/marketing/RULES.md` §8 checklist passed.
- [ ] Item moved to **Review** in Notion (Script & Record Videos → Steps).

## 6. Learning from the owner's edits

When the owner changes a script in the Review column, compare his version with ours and add the lesson here (one
line each, dated): a word he prefers, a phrase he cut, a gesture he added. Over time this section becomes the most
accurate description of his voice; it wins over the general rules above when they disagree (but never over
`docs/marketing/RULES.md`).
